# Atomic Sandbox joins a 17-year-old idea

*Novelty check, 21 October 2026. It is based on four research notes (interactive quantum tools, GPU DFT codes, on-the-fly ML potentials, public and educational tools). "Not found" means not found in this search. It does not mean "never done". The descriptions of Atomic Sandbox come from the project itself and have not been benchmarked independently.*

## Verdicts at a glance

**Genuinely new, to our knowledge (not found in this search)**

- **Real-space-grid Kohn–Sham DFT driving interactive, drag-driven Born–Oppenheimer MD.** Every interactive quantum tool we found uses something else: Gaussian-basis Hartree–Fock (the Martínez group's TeraChem interactive MD, 2015, and InteraChem, 2021–22), semi-empirical or tight-binding methods (SCINE Heron/Sparrow, Narupa), or small Gaussian-basis demos in the browser (webgpu-q). The nearest grid-based relative is **hf2d-lab**, a *2D* Hartree–Fock model.
- **A 3D Kohn–Sham SCF running on a graphics-API compute backend** (WGSL through wgpu, which maps to Vulkan, DX12 or Metal). We found no production DFT code that targets Metal, Vulkan or WebGPU. Two caveats apply. Vendor neutrality is a property of wgpu, and the project's own measurements are on one AMD card. Running on NVIDIA, Intel or Apple is a design property here, not a demonstrated one. The closest prior art is hf2d-lab (2D, WebGPU f32) and the older OpenCL backends in Octopus and BigDFT.
- **Abandoning a solve mid-flight when the user edits the scene, then warm-restarting.** No source describes this. TeraChem interactive MD runs every step to completion and freezes the display on slow steps. The Reiher group bridges slow solves with a "mediator potential" instead. Whether InteraChem interrupts solves is unknown.
- **An ML potential trained live while the user drags atoms, with every decision visible.** That covers fallbacks, audits, refits, user-triggered teaching passes and background DFT audits of accepted answers. The nearest precedent is Amabilino et al. 2019, where VR-sampled data trained a network *afterwards*, offline. No live GUI for active-learning decisions was found.

**New as a combination; the ingredients exist separately**

- **Live density and orbitals drawn straight from the solver's GPU memory during MD.** InteraChem already shows molecular orbitals in real time, so "first to show orbitals live" would be false. What is new is grid density drawn on the GPU without a CPU copy, on a non-CUDA GPU.
- **Chemistry with no per-element fitted parameters in an interactive tool.** TeraChem's Hartree–Fock is also first-principles. The difference is from the maintained open tools, which all use parameterised PM6, DFTB or xTB.
- **A multi-gate trust system:** Bayesian variance, a MaxVol extrapolation grade, novelty, class-wise leave-one-out trust, a lone-atom rule and live audits. Each gate has published precedent. All of them together, inside a GUI, was not found.
- **Approximation labels for non-experts.** These are more thorough than anything surveyed: MolCalc's "quite different from experiment" caveat, Falstad's "variational approximations" label and webgpu-q's diagnostics come closest. This is a matter of degree, not a first.

**Already done (closest prior art)**

- **Interactive quantum chemistry with dragged atoms:** the Reiher line since 2009 (SCINE Heron + Sparrow), Martínez (TeraChem interactive MD 2015, InteraChem) and Glowacki (Narupa, now NanoVer).
- **Warm-starting each SCF from the last step:** Luehr et al. 2015. **Keeping the display smooth during slow solves:** Luehr's interpolation and Reiher's mediator potential. **A user pull force inside the MD:** Luehr's haptic force and Narupa's user forces.
- **On-the-fly ML potentials with Bayesian uncertainty and DFT fallback:** VASP ML_FF (2019) and FLARE (2020). **A linear model with a MaxVol extrapolation grade:** MTP (2016–17) and pacemaker/ACE (2023). **Bayesian linear ACE with uncertainty-triggered DFT:** HAL (2022).
- **Single-precision GPU DFT:** DFT-FE (mixed precision). **DFT on AMD GPUs:** CP2K, GPAW, Octopus (HIP) and VASP 6.6 (experimental).
- **Live SCF in a browser on WebGPU** for a few small molecules: webgpu-q. This is self-reported and unverified.

The core idea of a "virtual molecular modelling kit with real electrons" is not new. The Martínez group described almost exactly that in 2015, and the Reiher group started in 2009. What Atomic Sandbox does differently is the *route*: grid DFT instead of semi-empirical shortcuts, a consumer GPU from any vendor instead of NVIDIA-only CUDA, solves that can be abandoned mid-flight, and an ML stand-in that learns in front of the user. As far as this search reaches, that route has no precedent. It is a defensible "first" as a combination. Most individual parts are not firsts.

## Three research lines built "drag the atoms, solve the electrons" since 2009

The longest-running line is the Reiher group's at ETH Zürich. It runs from "haptic quantum chemistry" (2009) through "real-time quantum chemistry" (2013) to today's **SCINE Heron**. Heron is a BSD-licensed GUI where a mouse or haptic device deforms a molecule and the energy responds at a **60 Hz visual / 1 kHz haptic target**, computed with DFTB, PM6-type or xTB methods from SCINE Sparrow ([Heron, arXiv:2406.09541](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.09541v1)). Heron 2.0.0 was released in August 2024 ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/scine-heron/)). The group has used the approach in teaching ([Weymuth & Reiher, CHIMIA 2021](https://www.chimia.ch/chimia/article/view/2021_045)).

The second line is the Martínez group at Stanford, and it is the closest ab initio precedent. Luehr, Jin and Martínez ran **restricted Hartree–Fock in small Gaussian bases on 8 NVIDIA GTX 970s**. A 24-atom caffeine molecule took 196–718 ms per 1 fs step. The SCF was warm-started from the previous step's density, and a haptic pull was integrated into the dynamics ([Luehr dissertation, ch. 7](https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:hb803mt5913/FinalDissertation-augmented.pdf); [JCTC 2015](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/10.1021/acs.jctc.5b00419)). That work listed DFT and "on-the-fly orbital display" as future work. Its successor, **InteraChem**, adds VR and tracks "molecular orbitals and bond order in real time" ([JCTC 2022](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/abs/10.1021/acs.jctc.2c00005)). InteraChem's default backend is xTB, and TeraChem handles "a dozen atoms" in real time ([Martínez software page](https://mtzweb.stanford.edu/software)). TeraChem is commercial and CUDA-only ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeraChem)).

The third line is Glowacki's **Narupa**, now **NanoVer**: open-source, multi-person VR molecular dynamics. Its reactive demos used SCINE Sparrow PM6 or DFTB+, reaching "100–200 atoms at interactive latencies". The paper does not mention density or orbital display ([O'Connor et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01827v3)). The current NanoVer papers describe OpenMM and ASE molecular dynamics, with no electronic structure confirmed ([arXiv:2606.30678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30678); [JOSS 2025](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.08118)).

Against these three lines, Atomic Sandbox's distinct engineering choice is clear. **Luehr runs every step to completion**: "for pathologically long MD steps … the displayed trajectory is frozen and haptic input ignored until the MD step completes" ([dissertation §7](https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:hb803mt5913/FinalDissertation-augmented.pdf)). Reiher's mediator potential stands in for the solve rather than abandoning it. Neither matches Atomic Sandbox's abandon-and-warm-restart on every edit. The trade-off cuts the other way on size, however. Semi-empirical tools handle 100–200 atoms at interactive rates, while Atomic Sandbox handles small molecules. Expect that gap from grid DFT; it is not a flaw to hide.

## No production DFT code runs on Metal, Vulkan or WebGPU

Every major GPU electronic-structure code found uses CUDA, HIP, OpenACC or OpenMP offload, aimed at datacentre GPUs. VASP 6.6 added experimental AMD and Intel support via OpenMP offload for MI250X/MI300A and Intel Max cards ([VASP wiki](https://vasp.at/wiki/GPU_ports_of_VASP)). CP2K, GPAW and Octopus support AMD through HIP ([CP2K](https://www.cp2k.org/gpu); [GPAW](https://gpaw.readthedocs.io/documentation/gpu.html); [Octopus](https://octopus-code.org/documentation/main/tutorial/unsorted/running_octopus_on_graphical_processing_units)). Quantum ESPRESSO's AMD path lives only in a fork; LUMI said in May 2026 there is "no AMD release of QE that we can support" ([LUMI](https://lumi-supercomputer.github.io/LUMI-EasyBuild-docs/q/QuantumESPRESSO/)). A targeted search found **no Kohn–Sham code on Apple GPUs**, and Metal lacks double precision ([neurolabusc/Metal](https://github.com/neurolabusc/Metal)). The nearest attempt at vendor neutrality was OpenCL. Octopus's docs call that backend "vendor independent" but say the non-CUDA version is untested ([Octopus v16](https://octopus-code.org/documentation/16/tutorial/unsorted/running_octopus_on_graphical_processing_units/)).

The browser projects are the nearest neighbours, and all of them date from 2026. **hf2d-lab** has the most similar architecture: real-space grid, an f64 Rust reference, an optional WebGPU f32 path, and Born–Oppenheimer MD that refuses unconverged steps. It is explicitly "model physics, not predictive three-dimensional chemistry", a 2D Hartree–Fock model limited to 16 nuclei ([hf2d-lab](https://github.com/jjjhenriksen/hf2d-lab)). **andeplane/quantum-molecules** (merged 17 October 2026) uses the same Rust + WGSL stack, but its SCF runs on the CPU; WGSL only draws the density ([PR #1](https://github.com/andeplane/quantum-molecules/pull/1)). **webgpu-q** says it recomputes a Hartree–Fock dipole live as you bend water. That claim is self-reported and undated, and we did not verify whether its drag demo runs on the GPU ([webgpu-q](https://abgunaydin-webgpu-q.static.hf.space/)). Note one naming point: Atomic Sandbox uses wgpu, a Rust implementation of the WebGPU API, natively over Vulkan, DX12 or Metal. It is a desktop app, not a browser page.

On precision, the evidence supports single precision in principle. DFT-FE does about 85 % of its arithmetic in FP32. Its energy and force errors from this are more than two orders of magnitude below the discretisation error ([DFT-FE 1.0](https://www.osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/1888932); [arXiv 2412.19322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.19322)). However, **no published benchmark of an all-f32-grid 3D real-space Kohn–Sham code was found**. Atomic Sandbox's own f32-versus-f64 validation is therefore unpublished evidence, and it would be worth publishing.

## Live-learning ML potentials exist, but never with a user watching

The learning loop itself is mature: predict, check the uncertainty, call DFT when it is too high, refit. **VASP ML_FF** has done this with Bayesian linear regression since 2019 ([Jinnouchi et al., arXiv 1903.09613](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.09613v1)). FLARE does it with sparse Gaussian-process variance ([Vandermause et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.02042v1)). Atomic Sandbox's extrapolation grade descends from **MTP's D-optimality/MaxVol** selection ([Podryabinkin & Shapeev](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.09346v3)) and from **pacemaker's** ACE active sets ([Lysogorskiy et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08716)). **HAL** already combines linear ACE, a Bayesian posterior and uncertainty-triggered DFT ([van der Oord et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04225)). None of these ingredients is new.

What differs is the setting. Most tools do not call DFT inside the trajectory at all. pacemaker tells you to run DFT "with the tools of your choice" ([docs](https://pacemaker.readthedocs.io/en/latest/pacemaker/active_learning/)), and i-PI and GPUMD write uncertain frames to a file ([i-PI 3.0](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15224); [GPUMD](https://gpumd.org/gpumd/input_parameters/active.html)). The interactive-MD community used VR sampling to train networks *afterwards*. That work also found the resulting networks weaker away from the paths users explored, a warning that applies directly to user-driven training ([Amabilino et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.05417)). We found no dashboard that monitors interatomic-potential training live.

Atomic Sandbox's background audits answer a documented weakness. VASP's own docs say its Bayesian error is "usually much smaller than the true error" once model error dominates ([VASP wiki](https://vasp.at/wiki/Machine_learning_force_field:_Theory)). Studies keep finding ML-potential uncertainties overconfident ([arXiv 2108.05748](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.05748); [Annevelink & Viswanathan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15653)). Continuous DFT re-checks of answers the model accepted give an empirical calibration check that the published on-the-fly codes do not seem to expose by default.

## Why hasn't this been built before?

The "simple idea" has in fact been pursued for 17 years. What was missing is this particular route to it. The reasons below separate what the notes show from what we infer.

**Evidence: speed targets pushed interactive tools to semi-empirical methods.** Reiher's group set 60 Hz visual and 1 kHz haptic targets, and reached them only with semi-empirical methods plus a mediator potential ([arXiv 1208.3717](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1208.3717)). The group judged interactivity feasible "for small molecular systems within a density functional theory model or … about 100 atoms within semi-empirical models" ([arXiv 1512.02111](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1512.02111)). It also named SCF convergence as the bottleneck. Earlier interactive DFT in Turbomole managed about one update per second for up to 8 atoms ([Luehr dissertation](https://stacks.stanford.edu/file/druid:hb803mt5913/FinalDissertation-augmented.pdf)). With 1 kHz as the goal, grid DFT was never a candidate. Atomic Sandbox accepts slower solves and hides them with dynamics pacing, cancellation and an ML stand-in, a different bargain.

**Evidence: GPU quantum chemistry is locked to vendors and to FP64.** The only GPU ab initio interactive engine, TeraChem, runs on NVIDIA CUDA only ([PetaChem](https://petachem.com/performance.html)). Production codes assume double precision; Octopus requires it and calls consumer GPUs "inferior" ([Octopus](https://octopus-code.org/documentation/main/tutorial/unsorted/running_octopus_on_graphical_processing_units)). Metal and WGSL offer no FP64 at all. *Inference:* a graphics-API backend becomes possible only once a code splits the work into f32 grid arithmetic on the GPU and f64 small linear algebra on the CPU. That design was not obvious until mixed-precision results such as DFT-FE's showed f32 was safe for the bulk of the work.

**Evidence: funding and prestige reward scale, not desktops.** GPU DFT effort targets supercomputers: DFT-FE's 2023 Gordon Bell run on Frontier ([summary](https://www.nextplatform.com/2024/01/10/sometimes-the-best-ai-accelerator-is-the-37888-gpus-you-already-have/)), and Intel support built for Aurora ([Codeplay](https://codeplay.com/portal/blogs/2024/10/24/argonne-national-labs-bring-oneapi-to-nwchemex-project)). Educational tools went the other way, toward zero-install classical models. PhET's sims use Lennard-Jones potentials ([PhET mirror](https://clixplatform.tiss.ac.in/phet/en/simulation/atomic-interactions.html)), and live molecular electron clouds appear nowhere in the public tools surveyed. *Inference:* a consumer-GPU, small-molecule DFT sandbox falls between these two incentive systems. It is too small for an HPC paper and too heavy for a classroom web app.

**Inference: the skills rarely meet in one team.** The project needs GPU and graphics programming, numerical DFT (SCF, pseudopotentials, forces), ML potentials with uncertainty, and non-expert interface design. The notes show these as separate communities. The interactive-MD groups plug in existing engines; the ML-potential groups run batch HPC workflows; the education groups use classical models. No source shows them meeting.

**Evidence of recency: the toolchain only just arrived.** Every browser/WebGPU electronic-structure project found dates from 2026: hf2d-lab, quantum-molecules and GANSU-Lite ([GANSU-Lite](https://github.com/Yasuaki-Ito/GANSU-Lite)). *Inference:* the cross-vendor compute layer these projects and Atomic Sandbox rely on is new. A comparable attempt in the OpenCL era stayed untested off NVIDIA.

**The other side of the argument.** Researchers may simply not need this. For reaction exploration, semi-empirical tools are faster and handle larger systems. LSDA has known errors (Atomic Sandbox's own H atom is −0.479 versus −0.5 Ha). A small-molecule grid code will not replace VASP or CP2K. Part of "why not before" may therefore be that nobody's job required it.

## What it means for researchers and for the public

**For researchers**, the novelty is methodological, and it is narrow but real. The publishable pieces are three. First, interactive grid Kohn–Sham BOMD on any vendor's consumer GPU, with cancellable warm-restarted solves. Second, an f32 grid with f64 subspace algebra, validated against oracles; no published benchmark of this was found. Third, live active learning with continuous audits, reported as measured calibration (for example, the fraction of audited answers outside k·σ). The research community will expect comparisons with TeraChem/InteraChem, SCINE Heron, hf2d-lab and HAL, and an honest account of system size. The engine is not a production competitor.

**For the general public**, the gap is larger. Public tools show either hydrogen-like clouds (Atom in a Box, Falstad), precomputed surfaces (PhET Molecule Polarity), or classical balls and springs. In the classical tools, bonds are put in by hand ([PhET Molecule Polarity guide](https://phet.colorado.edu/files/teachers-guide/molecule-polarity-html-guide_en.pdf); [Falstad](https://falstad.com/qmmo/directions.html)). A desktop app where a bond *forms* in the electron density as two atoms approach, with no per-element rules, was not found. Education research suggests the need is real. Students cling to the planetary Bohr model, and some reject electron clouds outright ([Tsaparlis et al. 2009](https://www.ssoar.info/ssoar/bitstream/document/13432/1/ssoar-ijse-2009-07-tsaparlis_et_al-high-school_students_conceptual_difficulties_and.pdf)). The same research warns that a better picture alone does not fix understanding. The engagement gains reported for VR molecular dynamics are engagement, not measured learning ([NanoVer teaching summary](https://chemistry.coe.edu/piper/posts/teaching-enzyme-catalysis/index.html)).

## Conclusion

The safe public claim is: "To our knowledge, the first interactive sandbox that solves real-space Kohn–Sham DFT live on any vendor's consumer GPU, cancels and restarts solves as you edit, and trains its ML stand-in in front of you." It should come with explicit credit to TeraChem interactive MD, InteraChem, SCINE Heron, Narupa, hf2d-lab and HAL. Claims to avoid are "first interactive quantum chemistry", "first live orbitals", "first on-the-fly ML potential" and "first WebGPU quantum MD". Each has clear prior art.

Two things would strengthen the "new" claims: demonstrated runs on NVIDIA, Intel and Apple hardware, which the notes do not show, and published f32-accuracy and audit-calibration numbers. Several gaps also remain open. We do not know how InteraChem moves its orbitals or whether it interrupts solves. NanoVer's current quantum backends are unconfirmed. Commercial tools (QuantumATK, MedeA, SAMSON) were not checked for live training GUIs. A code-level search of GitHub for WGSL Kohn–Sham projects was not possible. Any of these could narrow the verdicts above.
