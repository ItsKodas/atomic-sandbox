# Atomic Sandbox

An interactive 3D sandbox that simulates matter from the atomic level up with real physics, written from scratch in Rust + wgpu. Nuclei are classical point particles; electrons are solved with real-space Kohn-Sham density-functional theory on a GPU grid. Nothing chemical is hardcoded — every element is described by its atomic number and isotope mass only, and bonds, shapes and energies have to *emerge* from the equations.

![Water (57³ grid) with its electron density cloud, the menu bar, the toolbar and the dock](docs/images/ui_water.png)

## What it does today

| Milestone | Status | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| M1 — CPU reference solver (`qm`) | done | f64 spin-polarised LSDA Kohn-Sham solver on a uniform real-space grid, validated against NIST/literature atoms and the H₂ bond length |
| First pictures | done | H₂ density slice and energy curve (`docs/images/h2_*.png`) |
| M2 — 3D viewer (`app`, `render`) | done | place / select / drag nuclei, orbit camera, volume or isosurface density, energy readouts, scene save/load |
| M3 — GPU solver (`qm-gpu`) | done | the same solver in f32 WGSL compute, 30–100× faster, agrees with the CPU reference at fixed smearing to ≤ 3.5e-7 Ha for H … LiH and 4.1e-6 Ha for H₂O, forces to ≤ 3.7e-6 Ha/Bohr for H₂ and 4.8e-6 for H₂O; the viewer re-solves ~0.1–0.2 s after you move an atom |
| M4 — Dynamics (`sim`) | done | Born-Oppenheimer molecular dynamics in the viewer (velocity Verlet on the GPU Kohn-Sham forces, warm-started and density-extrapolated SCF with a cold retry on a stall): play / pause / step / rewind, drag a nucleus on a capped spring while it moves, pins, CSVR thermostat, temperature and energy readouts with a sparkline and an energy-drift health number; a GPU dynamics suite checks drift, vibration frequency, thermostat statistics and rewind |
| M5 — Surrogate ("estimated") force provider | done | a linear ACE-style model trained on this solver's own DFT, with a Bayesian uncertainty and DFT fallback, behind the same `ForceProvider` boundary: the **estimated** solver in the viewer samples the scene, then answers MD steps in ~5 µs and hands the step back to DFT wherever it is unsure; the surrogate validation suite checks it against its teacher (H₂ frequency within 7e-6 of DFT-MD, energies 2.5e-6 Ha / forces 2e-5 Ha/Bohr rms on fresh geometries, 38 DFT calls to learn H₂ from nothing) |
| M6 — Tools | done | bonds read off the solved density and drawn in the view, molecules labelled with their formula and name, a composition badge, isotopes (¹H ²H ³H … ²²Ne) per atom, a FIRE **Relax** tool, a periodic-table picker; a tools suite checks the bonds of H₂, He₂, H₃⁺ and water, the H₂/D₂ frequency ratio and the relax against an independent quench; the viewer's manual checklist became recorded input scripts that replay headlessly (`--record` / `--replay`) |

The sandbox now **moves**: the nuclei are classical particles pushed by the Kohn-Sham forces (velocity Verlet, 0.25 fs steps), and the electron cloud is re-solved at every step. It opens on H₂ at 1.6 Bohr, slightly compressed from the bond length of its smeared nuclei; press **Play** and it vibrates with a period of 10.3 fs (one DFT solve per step, while the window stays at full frame rate: on the RX 7900 XTX the viewer showed about 2.5 fs of simulated time per wall second at 49³ — the toolbar shows the live figure; the headless MD step of the validation suite is 63–82 ms, 3–4 fs/s). The dynamics suite measures what that run does against references: NVE energy drift −4.0e-6 Ha/ps (bound 2e-4), the vibration frequency against the curvature of the same energy surface to 5.6e-4, the thermostat's mean temperature, and rewind retracing to 5.8e-6 Bohr (`crates/validate/references.md`, "Dynamics (M4)").

![H₂ vibrating with a 300 K thermostat: the Graph tab shows E total and T](docs/images/ui_dock_graph.png)

**Estimated mode (M5).** Pick **estimated** as the solver and the viewer first *teaches* a surrogate on the scene: a sampling pass (about 40 s for H₂ at 49³ — 273 DFT solves at SCF tolerance 1e-6: isolated-atom references, random perturbations, a bond scan and a hot MD burst) fits a linear model of the energy with a Bayesian uncertainty. From then on each MD step asks the model; when its uncertainty is above the threshold, or the geometry is outside what it was taught, the step goes to DFT instead (a **fallback**, shown amber), and that answer becomes a new training sample — the model learns as you play. The validation suite (`crates/validate/tests/surrogate.rs`) measures it against its teacher: the H₂ vibration frequency within 7e-6 of the DFT-MD value, energies within 2.5e-6 Ha and forces within 2e-5 Ha/Bohr (rms) on fresh geometries, and an active-learning run from nothing that needs only 38 DFT calls in 300 steps. The model alone runs at **2.4 × 10⁵ steps/s** for H₂ (headless, the validate suite) against ~10 DFT steps/s. In the viewer a DFT **audit** every 2000 answered steps (a safety check: the teacher answers at that geometry and the gap is compared) runs on a second GPU teacher in the background while the model keeps stepping, so it no longer sets the pace: H₂ plays at ≈ 40 000 fs/s at speed 1000 (headless at 49³; 38 800 fs/s in the worker test at 41³, 44 000 with audits off — before the background auditor it was ≈ 3300 fs/s in the window). At that speed the model outruns the background teacher: the audits run back to back, one per DFT solve — every ≈ 20 000–40 000 answered steps rather than 2000 — and a failed one arrives that many steps after the geometry it checked (the Model tab's **audit lag**); the 2000-step cadence holds at paced speeds. The in-window rate has not been re-measured since; the window kept full frame rate before (p95 frame interval 5 ms). With the CPU teacher the audit still runs in the step. A surrogate step has no electrons, so the cloud between DFT densities is an **atomic superposition** — the teacher's own isolated atoms (solved once per species and grid in the background), placed at the current nuclei and blended with the last real density as the atoms move away from it — labelled "atomic superposition — not solved": it shows where the atoms are, not their bonds. The real densities come from DFT steps, from the background audits ("DFT density from the audit at t = … fs") and, if you switch it on, from a display-only **refresh** every 1–10 s while playing. With the superposition off, the last real density is drawn faded and labelled with its time. Both need the GPU teacher (the CPU teacher has no background solver).

![Estimated H₂: the model answers (green), the Readouts sparkline is coloured by who answered each step, the cloud is the last DFT density](docs/images/ui_main.png)

**Bonds, molecules and isotopes (M6).** After every converged DFT step the viewer reads the bonds off the density itself: a pair is bonded where the solved density piles up between the two nuclei beyond what their free atoms would put there — compared against the solver's own isolated atoms, solved once per element in the background (O ≈ 1 min at 57³; the status chip says "bond references: O 2/3…") — and nothing about chemistry is told to it. Water gets its two O–H bonds and no H–H bond, He₂ gets none (closed shells push density out of the contact), H₃⁺ gets a three-centre ring (dashed violet). Each molecule is labelled in the view ("H₂O — water") and listed in the Selection tab, and hovering an atom with the Select tool shows its badge: "¹⁶O · 8 p · 8 n · |F| 0.342 Ha/Bohr". **Relax** (toolbar, or Physics ▸ Relax geometry) moves the atoms downhill with FIRE until every force is below 1e-3 Ha/Bohr; under the estimated solver it relaxes on the model and checks the result with one DFT solve. Any atom can be swapped to another isotope (deuterium, tritium, ¹³C, ¹⁸O, …): only its mass changes, so D₂ vibrates slower than H₂ by √(m_D/m_H) — the tools suite measures 1.4136050 against 1.4136703 (−4.6e-5).

![Water: two O–H bonds read off the density, its label, and the badge of the selected oxygen](docs/images/m6_water_bonds.png)

Measured on an AMD RX 7900 XTX (one solve after a drag release): H₂ on a 49³ grid 0.2 s cold / 0.1 s warm; H₂O on 97³ 3.6 s. Energies vs the f64 CPU reference at fixed smearing (σ = h = 0.25, 49³): H 2.2e-8, He 5.5e-8, H₂ 3.3e-8, LiH 3.5e-7, H₂O 4.1e-6 Ha (bound 2e-5; the GPU's own H₂O path scatter is 5.4e-6); forces ≤ 3.7e-6 Ha/Bohr for H₂ (bound 5e-6) and 4.8e-6 for H₂O (bound 6e-5).

## Running it

Requirements: Rust (stable, 1.99 or newer), a Vulkan/DX12/Metal-capable GPU for the GPU backend (the CPU backend works anywhere, slowly).

```bash
cargo run -p app --release
```

Options: `-- --preset h2o` (presets: `h`, `h2`, `he`, `lih`, `h2o`), `-- --backend cpu` (the f64 reference: one MD step takes seconds), `-- --estimated` (start on the estimated solver: its sampling pass begins at once), `-- --speed 1000` (the steps-per-tick slider's start value), `-- --audit 200` (the estimated solver's DFT audit interval; default 2000, 0 = never). Always build `--release`; debug builds are far too slow for the solver.

### Controls

The window has four parts (screenshots below): a **menu bar** at the top (File, Scene, View, Physics, Help — what you set once — plus the scene chip and the control search), a **toolbar** at the bottom (what your hands do: time, speed, dt, tool, element, the selection's actions, the solver), a **dock** on the right (what you read: Readouts, Graph, Model, Selection and Log tabs; click the open tab's icon, or the chevron, to fold it to its icon strip — the strip's dots keep the drift, the model's trust and the error count visible, and hovering an icon peeks its key numbers), and the 3D view with a status chip (top left), the atom card (top right), toasts (bottom right) and a Volume / Isosurface switch with an axes gizmo (bottom left). Below 1240 points of width the toolbar and the menu bar switch to a compact layout (the toolbar already below about 1325, where its fixed-width groups stop fitting; the five menus fold into one, the element chips into one chip with a periodic-table flyout).

### Recording and replaying input

For contributors: the viewer's checklist runs headlessly as **replay scripts** (`crates/app/replays/*.replay`). `cargo run -p app --release -- --record session.replay` writes what you do in the window — the 3D view's mouse and keys, and every toolbar / menu / dock action by name (`action relax`, `action isotope 1 2`; the egui layout itself is not recorded) — with `wait ms` for pauses longer than 50 ms and `wait idle` whenever the solver went idle. `cargo run -p app --release -- --replay session.replay [--preset h2] [--backend gpu] [--screenshot last.png] [--size 1400x900]` plays one without a window and exits with code 1 at the first failed `expect` line, naming the line and what it saw. Add `expect` lines by hand (`nuclei 3`, `dist 0 1 1.75 1.83` in Bohr, `bonds 2`, `molecule 0 H2O`, `isotope 0 2`, `relaxed true`, `paused true`, `selected 1`, `source surrogate`, `no-error`) and let dynamics advance with `wait steps N` / `wait fs X`, never with wall time, so a script passes on a slower GPU too. Cursor positions are stored relative to the 3D view (offsets from its centre in half its height), so a script recorded at one window size picks the same atoms at another (camera orbits are per pixel, so the replayer lays the chrome out at the script's `view` size unless `--size` says otherwise). `cargo test -p app --release` runs every script (`replays_pass`); the format is documented in `crates/app/src/replay.rs`.

## Physics, honestly

- **Model:** Born-Oppenheimer separation; electrons in spin-polarised Kohn-Sham DFT with the LSDA exchange-correlation functional (Dirac exchange + Perdew–Wang 92 correlation); 8th-order finite-difference Laplacian; nuclei represented as narrow Gaussians (σ = grid spacing by default) with an analytic potential; Hartree potential from a multipole-bounded Poisson solve; Hellmann-Feynman forces.
- **Units:** atomic units everywhere inside the solver (Hartree, Bohr, mₑ, ħ = e = 1); the UI converts.
- **Precision:** f32 on the GPU grid with two-level compensated reductions; f64 on the CPU for nuclei, small dense algebra and the reference solver.
- **Known gaps, documented rather than hidden:** LSDA underbinds the hydrogen atom (−0.4777 Ha here vs −0.5 exact; the LSDA reference value is −0.4787); absolute energies of anything containing oxygen or heavier are not physical at the default 0.25 Bohr spacing because the nucleus is heavily smeared — the density *shapes* are right, the numbers are not; the f32 GPU path carries ~1e-6 Ha of scatter between solve paths; the lithium atom oracle misses its reference by 0.33 Ha at the tested grid and is kept as an ignored test. A stretched H₂ beyond ~3 Bohr keeps a bond line and a too-high energy (≈ +0.05 Ha at dissociation) because the solver stays on the spin-symmetric solution; the broken-symmetry fix (a spin-asymmetric start for even electron counts, keeping the lower energy) is a planned solver item. See `crates/validate/references.md` for every reference number and tolerance.
- **Dynamics on the smeared surface:** the vibrating H₂ runs at 3237 cm⁻¹ (period 10.30 fs) against 4401 cm⁻¹ measured, because at σ = h = 0.25 the smeared nuclei lengthen the bond (1.79 Bohr, vs 1.45 at σ → 0) and soften its well — the dynamics match the curvature of that same surface to 6e-4, and this solver's σ → 0 curvature gives ≈ 4405 ± 60 cm⁻¹, a figure too coarse to tell experiment (4401) from LSDA (4201).
- **Water heats up on Play:** the same smearing makes water's minimum on this surface near-linear (O–H 2.34 Bohr, 177°) and 0.160 Ha (4.4 eV) below the physical geometry the `h2o` preset loads, so pressing Play on `h2o` heats the molecule — past 10 000 K within ~2.5 fs while it opens toward linear; a 300 K thermostat (τ = 10 fs) brings it back to ~300 K after ~100 fs. Along that fast motion the warm-started solver sometimes stalls; it then retries the step cold (twice in 600 steps, the slower step 24 s instead of ~1 s at 57³), and only a failed cold retry pauses the run.
- **Draft mode guesses.** With "guess instead of falling back" on, the model answers where it would otherwise refuse, and what it says there is an extrapolation of a linear fit: beyond its 6 Bohr descriptor cutoff a bond contributes nothing at all (a pulled-apart pair is two free atoms at the sum of their reference energies, whatever the true dissociation curve does), and a geometry, species or charge state it never saw is answered confidently wrong — its uncertainty can even look small there (a linear model's variance shrinks where its features vanish). The energy bookkeeping stays honest (one surface, booked jumps); the forces do not. A region whose samples were quarantined (the model cannot represent them) is answered by the model too and re-learned only from the audits there, so it learns slowly; and on a hot draft run the drift readout can turn red — the guessed surface may be stiffer than the time step resolves. Steps answered by a guess are drawn red in the sparkline. Use it to look, not to measure.
- **The estimated solver is a model of this DFT, not of nature.** It inherits every approximation of its teacher (smearing, LSDA, the box) and adds its own fit error; the uncertainty it shows is a *model* uncertainty — how sure it is of reproducing the DFT — not a physical error bar. It is valid only where it has been taught: it falls back to DFT when unsure, and an untrusted model (too few samples, or a training sample it cannot fit to the accuracy it claims) never answers. A sample the model cannot represent at all is **quarantined** rather than allowed to untrust everything: kept in the store and the model file, excluded from the fit, its neighbourhood answered by DFT, and counted in the Model tab ("N samples quarantined — the model cannot represent them"); a later DFT answer there that agrees with the model releases it, and if three consistent DFT answers there all disagree with the model, one is kept and the next refit decides whether the model can learn that region. A DFT answer whose density on the outermost grid plane (one grid step inside the box wall) exceeds 2e-5 e/Bohr³ at the default 0.25 Bohr spacing — scaled by (h / 0.25 Bohr)², because the confinement energy per unit of that density grows as 1/h² (a quarter of it is tolerated at h = 0.125) — is used for that step but never trained on, and an audit there is recorded but not judged: the box's confinement (≈ π d ρ_face / (4κ²h²) for a nucleus d from the wall, ≈ 50 Ha per e/Bohr³ for H₂ at h = 0.25 — measured 40–55 — so ≥ 1e-3 Ha from there on) depends on *where* the molecule sits and how it is *oriented* against the box axes, which a model of the internal geometry neither can nor should learn — in the 10 Bohr box of 41³ that is H₂ stretched beyond ≈ 2.5 Bohr or parked 2–3 Bohr from a face, at the default 49³ beyond ≈ 4 Bohr; the cure is a bigger box. Such answers taken while a model is still being built — the sampling pass's own included — are never quarantined and leave the fit once enough others are held. The estimated solver's own DFT teacher (sampling pass, fallbacks, audits) solves at SCF tolerance 1e-6, tighter than the plain DFT backends' 1e-5, because its answers are kept as training data; a model taught at another tolerance is refused, never mixed. Without a model (a pass waiting to be started), DFT steps keep at most 4 Q′ samples before keeping one in ten. (Before Task 9 such an answer could fail an audit on pure confinement, get quarantined, and fence the equilibrium bond everywhere in the cell: a "trusted" model falling back on every step.) A trusted model that keeps falling back at one place says so in the Model tab. Repeated fallbacks at one geometry keep only one sample in ten. On a small 33³ grid (8 Bohr box) the teacher's own scatter from confinement keeps a short sampling pass from earning trust; the default 49³ is fine.
- **Bonds are read off the density, with stated limits.** A pair is bonded when the corrected density between the nuclei has a saddle (or, when a heavy atom's smeared tail swamps the saddle, a maximum of the deformation density) above a floor of 0.01 e/Bohr³, where the density exceeds the free atoms' superposition by at least 11 % (δ = 0.11, three times the measured noise), the point lies in the pair's own share of space (Hirshfeld weights), and the feature is a maximum across the line — or, if it rises sideways into a non-nuclear point, a multi-centre ring bond (H₃⁺). The criteria follow Bader's bond critical point; the detector is a line probe, not full QTAIM. Measured on this surface: H₂ q 1.70 (bonded), He₂ q 0.97 (not), water's O–H bonds and no H–H bond (a neighbouring O–H lobe seen from the side is rejected by the share-of-space test), LiH ρ_b 0.042 (literature ≈ 0.04), and HeH⁺ bonded although the spec expected a miss for this closed-shell ion (`crates/validate/references.md`, "Tools (M6 Task 7)"). Known artefacts: a stretched H₂ keeps its bond line up to ≈ 5 Bohr, because the solver stays on the spin-symmetric solution (the same caveat as its energy above); at h = 0.25 heavy atoms are smeared, so the bond topology is right but the bond-point numbers are not physical — the smeared nucleus even reorders oxygen's shells (2s above 2p), and its free-atom reference is a closed-shell singlet because the triplet does not converge on this grid. Names come from a small table of trivial names (water, ammonia, …) or IUPAC rules for binary compounds, marked "(systematic)" — or, by the name-style setting, IR-5 stoichiometric names ("dihydrogen monoxide") or IR-6 parent hydrides ("oxidane"); no general organic nomenclature. They are labels, never fed back into the physics. H₃ is not a stable molecule; H₃⁺ is.
- **Isotopes change the mass, nothing else.** The Born-Oppenheimer surface does not depend on nuclear mass, so swapping an isotope changes the kinetic energy (booked) and the dynamics, never the forces. D₂/H₂: measured ω ratio 1.4136050 vs √(m_D/m_H) = 1.4136703 from the AME2020 atomic masses (−4.6e-5); experiment's 1.41268 differs by the non-Born-Oppenheimer corrections no BO code reproduces.
- **Relax is FIRE** (Bitzek et al. 2006, with Guénolé et al. 2020's half-step backtrack), stopping at max\|F\| < 1e-3 Ha/Bohr; on H₃⁺ it reaches the same minimum as an independent velocity quench to 1.8e-6 Ha. On a small molecule it is not faster than the quench (25 vs 16 evaluations): its adaptive step pays off on many soft modes, not three stiff ones. On the default grid the relaxed water is near-linear (the smearing artefact above).
- **No per-element parameters.** Allowed inputs are Z and the isotope mass (plus display colours and radii, which are not physics). No force fields, no fitted pair potentials, no chemistry libraries.

## Roadmap after M5

Scaling up the "ladder": more elements (pseudopotentials for Z > 10), larger systems through ML potentials trained on this solver's own data and loaded as data behind the same `ForceProvider` boundary, then coarse-grained and continuum stages. Gravity exists as an external-field hook but is invisible at atomic scale, as it should be.


Nuclear physics (decay, transmutation) is planned as a **nuclear-events layer**, not as explicit protons and neutrons: the nucleus is 10⁻⁵ of the atom and no first-principles route from QCD exists, so nuclei stay points for the electrons while a stochastic α/β/γ event process — Q-values from the isotope mass table, rates from measured half-lives — changes Z live and lets the chemistry respond. Decay time gets its own labelled clock, separate from the MD speed.
