## 1. Purpose

M6 gives the sandbox the *reading* tools a chemist expects on top of the physics that already runs: bonds and molecules read off the electron density (never off a distance table), formulas and names for what the density says is there, isotopes as a per-nucleus mass number, a one-click relaxation to find shapes, a composition badge that shows the nucleus as data, and an input-replay harness so the viewer's manual checklist stops being manual. It also pays the structural debts M4/M5 deferred: the force providers leave `sim` for their own crate behind a `Trainable` trait, the two 3 k-line files are split, the automatic sampling pass is sized from a median, and the far-drag regression is re-pinned at 49³.

Nothing in M6 adds physics to the simulation. Every tool either reads the state (bonds, names, badge), re-labels an input the physics already takes (isotope mass — one of the two allowed per-element inputs), or drives the existing provider (relax). Rule 1 holds throughout: the only per-element inputs remain Z and measured isotope masses; names, symbols and colours are display data.

### What success looks like

Build water from an O and two H; after the solve converges two thin lines appear (O–H, O–H, never H–H), the label reads **H₂O — water**; press **Relax** and the molecule settles to the minimum of the σ = 0.25 surface with the energy it shed shown as work done by the tool; swap one H for ²H in the picker and the badge reads "²H · 1 p · 1 n"; in the validation suite D₂ vibrates slower than H₂ by √(m_D/m_H) = 1.41367 on the same surface; a recorded session of the viewer checklist replays headlessly and asserts what the checklist used to ask a person to look at.

## 2. Non-goals

- **Periodic cells, pressure, pseudopotentials, Z > 10** — the next milestones (user-requests note, "Beyond M5"). The VSEPR angles (H₂O 104.5°, NH₃ 106.7°, CH₄ 109.5°) are the *oracle of the pseudopotential milestone*; M6's relax tool is what will measure them, and at h = 0.25 all-electron it will find water near-linear (documented resolution limit, M4 handoff). The relax validation here uses H₃⁺ and H₂.
- **Nuclear events** (decay, transmutation, the nuclear clock) — the later "nuclear events" milestone; M6's isotopes and badge are its inputs.
- **Bader basin charges and bond orders.** Molecular charges are not partitioned (§4.1 rule for the charge superscript); bond lines carry no order. Bond *strength* is reported as the density at the bond point, nothing more. No charge-dependent rescaling of the promolecule either (§3.6).
- **Hover/select pixel-driving of the egui panel in replays** — panel actions are replayed by name (§8), not by pixel.
- **The `ui.rs` split** — that file is being restructured on the UI-redesign branch (user-requests note, "UI redesign"); M6 does not touch its layout (§10).
- **The energy-relative box rule (κ from the HOMO eigenvalue) and the spectral Σ / warm evidence for S = 4** — surrogate performance items from the M5 handoff; they ride to the pseudopotential milestone, where S = 4 (C, N, O, H) becomes usable.
- **Smooth density crossfade, draft mode, container walls** — queued on the viewer-extras branch (`m5b-viewer-extras`), not here.

## 3. Bond detection from the density

### 3.1 The physics

Bader's theory of atoms in molecules (Bader, *Atoms in Molecules: A Quantum Theory*, Oxford 1990) defines a bond by the topology of ρ: two nuclei are linked by a bond path when a (3, −1) critical point of ρ — a minimum of ρ along the path between them and a maximum in the two directions across it — lies between them and its gradient paths terminate at both nuclei. The density at that bond critical point, ρ_b, measures the bond: covalent bonds have ρ_b ≳ 0.2 e/Bohr³, closed-shell contacts (ionic, hydrogen-bonded, van der Waals) ≲ 0.1 (Bader & Essén, J. Chem. Phys. 80, 1943 (1984)). A bond path also exists between two *repelling* closed-shell atoms (He₂ at any separation has a (3, −1) point between the nuclei — the "bond path ≠ bond" discussion), so topology alone does not say "bonded"; what does is whether the density between the atoms is *accumulated* relative to the atoms merely sitting next to each other.

M6's detector approximates the bond path by the straight internuclear segment (the chord) and decides each candidate pair (A, B) within R_max = 8 Bohr by three element-agnostic conditions on the **bias-corrected** density ρ̂ (§3.3: the provider's trilinear sample divided by the interpolation bias the same operator puts on the promolecule — the review's MUST-1) and the **promolecule** ρ_pro (§3.2: the sum over *all* nuclei of the spherically averaged isolated-neutral-atom density of that species, solved by this solver at the scene's own grid and σ). Every constant is universal and recorded with its derivation in `references.md`.

1. **A bond point on the chord.** Sample ρ̂ at M = 33 points s_k = k/32 along R_A → R_B and take one of two routes:
   - **Route S (saddle):** the interior minimum (k = 1 … 31) lies below both endpoints → p* is there (`s` = s_k). Across the chord, for two unit vectors e₁, e₂ ⊥ (R_B − R_A), require ρ̂(p* ± h e_k) ≤ (1 + ε_⊥) ρ̂(p*). If that fails, the rise is followed before the pair is rejected (**the multi-centre march**, MUST-2, re-review R-1): the march is a **gradient ascent, re-steered at every step** — never a straight line, which is not rotation-invariant (the water-like fixture rotated 45° about its H–H axis sends a straight march 0.79 Bohr past O and manufactures an H–H bond). The first direction is the transverse gradient estimated from the four round-2 probes, g⊥ = Σ_k [ρ̂(p* + h e_k) − ρ̂(p* − h e_k)] e_k; each step of h/2 then re-estimates ∇ρ̂ from ±h/2 probes on the three axes (7 samples per step: the point itself — stop (ii) compares ρ̂ at the new point with the last on the same operator — and the six probes; its magnitude g_j is kept) and turns along it. The march stops at the first of: **(i)** the next point lies within h of a nucleus → the rise was a neighbour's hill → `NotATransverseMaximum`; **(ii)** ρ̂ decreases → a non-nuclear maximum; **(iii)** g_j < f_g·g_0 with f_g = 0.25 → a non-nuclear stationary point reached (a ring point whose in-plane curvature is nearly flat: the regime the reviewer's LCAO estimate puts H₃⁺ in, where the ring point and the bond points are degenerate within a few per cent, and where a "ρ decreases" test alone can slip past the centre and run on to the opposite nucleus); **(iv)** 2R_max/h steps → `NotATransverseMaximum`. Ends (ii) and (iii) keep the pair as a candidate flagged `multi_centre`. The rule is a statement about ρ̂'s gradient field only: a rise that ends at or stalls on a non-nuclear point is a ring or cage point; one that runs into a nucleus is that nucleus's hill. On the analytic fixtures it ends 0.24 Bohr from O for the water-like density at 0°, 45°, 90° and a random rotation (verdict identical), and 1.1–1.2 Bohr from every nucleus (0.1–0.25 Bohr from the centroid) for the H₃⁺-like density at bump heights from +8 % to +15 %, flat or tilted.
   - **Route D (deformation; the smeared X–H endpoint rule, MUST-4):** no interior minimum below both endpoints — at σ = h = 0.25 a heavy nucleus's smeared tail can overwhelm a hydrogen's smeared cusp, so ρ runs monotonically from O down to H and the saddle degenerates onto the H nucleus (Kato's cusp, which makes every nucleus a maximum along every line in the exact density, is smeared away). Then the bond point is the interior maximum of the **deformation ratio** q(s) = ρ̂(s)/ρ_pro(s) along the chord — where the atoms put more density than they would alone; it must be a genuine interior maximum (q(p*) above both endpoint values, else `NoInteriorMinimum`), and the transverse test is applied to q: q(p* ± h e_k) ≤ (1 + ε_⊥) q(p*).
   Route S is used whenever an interior minimum of ρ̂ exists; Route D only otherwise — one deterministic answer per pair. Both are statements about densities only.
2. **A floor.** ρ̂(p*) ≥ ρ_floor = 1e-2 e/Bohr³. Below Bader's closed-shell range nothing is a bond; two hydrogens 5 Bohr apart (ρ_mid ≈ 2e⁻⁵/π = 4.3e-3) are not bonded, two at 2.5 Bohr (ρ_mid ≈ 0.052 before accumulation) are candidates. A universal classification constant like the surrogate's 2e-5 box rule, not a per-element number.
3. **Accumulation.** q(p*) = ρ̂(p*)/ρ_pro(p*) ≥ 1 + δ. Covalent bonds accumulate density between the nuclei (exact H₂ at 1.4 Bohr: 0.27 against a promolecule of 0.157 — ratio 1.72; H₂ at its σ = 0.25 R_e is similar, measured in §11); closed-shell contacts *deplete* it (He₂ at 3.0 Bohr: the promolecule at the midpoint is 0.039, above the floor, and Pauli repulsion pushes the molecular density below it — the discriminator the floor alone cannot be).
4. **Attribution** (added by Task 7 from the real water surface, ruling P11). At p* the pair's own atoms are the two largest terms of the promolecule: no third nucleus C has ρ̄_C(|p* − R_C|) > min(ρ̄_A(|p* − R_A|), ρ̄_B(|p* − R_B|)) — else `NeighbourRegion { atom: C }`. Checked last, only for a pair that passed 1–3. **Why:** on the smeared water surface (57³) the H–H chord has no interior minimum (oxygen's valence fills its middle), takes Route D, and finds q's interior maximum 0.18 Bohr from one H (q 1.43, transverse ratio on q 1.02, ρ̂ 0.25): conditions 1–3 all pass and the first detector drew an H–H bond. That maximum is the **O–H bond's own deformation lobe** — the O–H chord finds its q maximum 0.23 Bohr from the same H (q 1.47); the two chords leave the H 38° apart and the H–H chord passes 0.139 Bohr (under one grid spacing) from the O–H point, so the ±h transverse probes see a 2 % rise, inside ε_⊥. What separates the two is whose density is there: at the H–H point the promolecular terms are H₁ 0.111, O 0.064, H₂ 0.002 (O thirty-two times the partner); at the O–H point H₁ 0.107, O 0.080, H₂ 0.002. A bond point of A–B lies on the interatomic surface where A's and B's basins meet (Bader 1990); the promolecule's stockholder shares (Hirshfeld, Theor. Chim. Acta 44, 129 (1977)) are the parameter-free estimate of whose space a point is, so a point where a third atom outweighs the weaker partner is the third atom's — a bond of its own seen from the side. No constant (rule 1): two numbers the detector already has are compared; rotation-invariant; real-surface margins 30–45× both ways. It also rejects the end-to-end chord of a linear A–B–C, whose interior minimum is the A–B bond point. **Rejected alternatives:** an exclusion radius about each nucleus tied to σ and h — the legitimate O–H point (0.23 Bohr from H) and the spurious H–H point (0.18) differ by 0.05 Bohr in their distance from H (0.14 Bohr apart in space), both inside one smearing width, so any radius between them would be fitted to water; the singlet O reference as the cause — scaling the O table by 0.9 / 1.1 leaves the H–H pair bonded (q 1.49 / 1.37). Pinned by `sim::bonds` `a_neighbours_bond_seen_from_the_side_is_not_the_pairs` (a fixture of the real surface's shape, the scan computing condition 4 independently from the analytic atom terms, rotated) and the water oracle (§11).

**The constants and the noise they must clear.** Trilinear interpolation of an atomic tail e^{−κr} on a 0.25 Bohr grid is biased by +κ²h²/8 at a cell midpoint: +3.1 % for hydrogen (κ = 2), +9.0 % for helium (κ = 3.4) — the review's table — and the bias depends on where the sample point sits in its cell. A raw ratio of two such samples (ρ at p*, the promolecule from directional samples) would carry several per cent of position-dependent error, comparable to δ and above a 2 % ε_⊥: the first draft of this spec claimed ~1e-3 and was wrong by an order of magnitude. The same-operator construction of §3.3 removes the leading term: ρ and ρ_pro are both interpolated by the *same* trilinear weights at the *same* points, so their biases cancel to the difference of their local curvatures, (h²/8)·|∂²ρ/ρ − ∂²ρ_pro/ρ_pro| along the chord — second order in the deformation density. **Measured (re-review, the gridded bonded-pair fixture on a 49³ h = 0.25 grid over 24 placements):** the corrected q spreads by 0.051 (≈ 3 % of 1.82; the raw ratio spreads by 0.142, so the correction removes two thirds), the transverse ratio by 0.022, and the corrected mean sits 2.6 % below the exact fixture's — a systematic in the safe direction. So the honest noise figure is **≈ 3 %**, not the "≲ 2 %" first claimed. **Provisional constants under the ≥ 3× rule: δ = 0.10 (≥ 3 × 0.028, rounded) and ε_⊥ = 0.07 (≥ 3 × 0.022)**; Task 3's gridded fixture re-measures the spreads with sanity ceilings (q ≤ 0.06, transverse ≤ 0.03, |mean − exact| ≤ 0.06) and records them, Task 7's H₂ oracle measures them on the real surface, and a constant is raised — never lowered — if a measurement demands. The He₂ oracle asserts its depletion with that margin, not against 1.0. A consequence of ε_⊥ = 0.07: a transverse rise of a few per cent (the reviewer's LCAO estimate for H₃⁺) passes the transverse test outright and never reaches the march; the march is for rises above 7 %, and the fixture that exercises it uses a +11 % rise.

**Why the full promolecule and not ρ_A + ρ_B** (the brief's wording): with only the pair's own atoms, the H–H chord in water compares ρ at its minimum (oxygen's valence density, ≈ 0.3) with 2ρ_H ≈ 0.04 — a ratio of 8 and a false H–H bond. The sum over all atoms is the promolecule of the deformation-density literature and makes the test mean what it says. On the smeared surface the H–H chord through oxygen's hill is rejected by whichever condition applies — no interior minimum below the H endpoints (`NoInteriorMinimum`), or an interior minimum whose transverse rise toward O ends *at the O nucleus* (`NotATransverseMaximum`: the march distinguishes a neighbour's hill from a ring point), or no accumulation — and the water oracle records which (§11). In a charged scene the neutral promolecule over-counts electrons by |charge| (H₃⁺: three neutral H have one electron too many, HeH⁺ likewise), which lowers every ratio; §3.6 says what that costs and why it is not corrected.

**What this is not.** Not QTAIM: no gradient-path integration, no basins, no bond order, no ellipticity. The chord is exact for symmetric bonds and close for the nearly straight bond paths of the small molecules in range; the multi-centre march is a one-dimensional probe of the ring/cage point, not a critical-point search. Ionic and charged closed-shell bonds are the open validation question (§3.6).

### 3.2 Isolated-atom references

The promolecule needs ρ̄_Z(r), the spherical average of the isolated neutral atom's density. The surrogate's sampling pass already solves exactly these atoms (one nucleus at the cell centre, every allowed multiplicity solved cold, the lowest energy kept — ruling S9; `sim::sampling` module doc, "References") and keeps only their energies. M6 keeps their **radial density** too, built by **shell binning the grid itself** (MUST-1): the atom sits on the centre node of the odd-n cell, so after the solve the density is read at every grid node within r_max = half-extent − 3h of the centre (`sample_density` at node positions — exact values, no interpolation; one readback of ≤ n³ floats, once per species) and binned by |r − R|, bins of width dr = h/2, each non-empty bin contributing (mean r, mean ρ); the table is the ordered list of non-empty bins (empty bins at small r — there is no node between r = 0 and r = h — are simply absent), r = 0 is the centre node alone, and evaluation is **log-linear** interpolation in r between neighbouring entries (exact for an exponential tail; the error is second order in the local change of the log-slope, far below the 0.8–2 % a linear table at dr would carry), 0 beyond the last entry.

- `RadialDensity { r: Vec<f64>, log_rho: Vec<f64> }` with `at(r)`; `electrons()` = 4π ∫ r² ρ̄ dr (trapezoid) as a sanity check, not physics.
- `AtomReference { energy: f64, multiplicity: u32, tol: f64, provider: &'static str, radial: RadialDensity }` keyed by `ReferenceKey { n, h, sigma, z }` in `sim::references::AtomReferences`: the radial table is a property of the grid and σ (a 1e-5 vs 1e-6 solve differs at the 1e-5 level, irrelevant to a 10 % test), so one entry per species serves both DFT backends and the surrogate's teacher; the energy is recorded with the tolerance and provider it was solved at, and the surrogate uses it only when they match its own (ruling S9's energy provenance is unchanged — it re-solves otherwise). `sim::references::solve_atom(provider, cell, sigma, z, mass, should_abort)` is the one routine that produces an entry; the sampling pass and the driver both call it.
- **Who solves them and when.** The driver (`Dynamics`) owns the cache. Whenever the scene's species set gains a species without an entry (scene load, `AddNucleus`, a grid or σ change) — once the provider has shown a density, which a Kohn-Sham provider does in its first evaluation — the driver queues one reference job per missing species. **References are side jobs, never steps** (Task 3 review I2, replacing the first draft's "one solve per tick on the stepping provider, then a cold step"): each species' scan (every multiplicity cold, the lowest energy kept with its table) runs off the stepping path, one species at a time, polled by the driver's ticks: under the GPU surrogate in its **background auditor's job slot** (`Trainable::request_atom_reference`/`take_atom_references`, beside the M5b isolated-atom densities; a Forget or an installed model abandons it, and the driver asks once more), otherwise on the provider's **side solver** — `ForceProvider::side_solver`, a fresh instance of the same solver at the same tolerance: a second `GpuKs` on the device, a second `CpuKs` (single-threaded: another core; the CPU surrogate lends its teacher's) — on **its own thread**. No job starts while a sampling pass runs; a failure is noted and not retried. So the stepping provider is never reset for a reference and its warm state survives every edit; no MD step is cold on their account; no command waits for an atom solve (Pause, Cancel and edits land within the running step's own abort bound, and an uncancellable CPU solve blocks nothing); the dynamics neither pause nor skip steps; while a species is missing the bond graph is absent and the snapshot says `bonds_pending = [Z, …]`. When the last entry lands the graph is read at once from the stepping provider's own density, even while paused. On the `h2o` preset at 57³ the O reference is three cold solves on the side solver while the run goes on — **measured ≈ 70 s** at tol 1e-5 (2.4 + 19 + 49 s: the singlet converges, the triplet and quintet stop unconverged at the 200-iteration cap, so the singlet is kept — `references.md`, "Tools (M6)"; ≈ 57 s at the validation suite's 1e-6), not the ≈ 1 s per solve first estimated. Entries persist for the session; a loaded sidecar seeds the surrogate's energies (the radial table is solved once when first needed — the sidecar format does not carry it; the driver's own cache is not seeded by it, review M7).

### 3.3 Sampling ρ, and the same operator on both sides

`ForceProvider` gains one optional method:

```rust
/// The latest converged density ρ↑ + ρ↓ (e/Bohr³) of this provider at arbitrary points (Bohr,
/// trilinear interpolation on its grid; 0 outside), or `None` when the provider holds no density
/// (a surrogate-answered step, an analytic provider, nothing solved yet).
fn sample_density(&mut self, points: &[[f64; 3]]) -> Option<Vec<f64>> { None }
```

- `CpuKs`: trilinear in f64 on `KsState::rho()` (`qm::Grid::trilinear`, the CPU reference).
- `GpuKs`: a WGSL kernel `sample_density` over the solver's padded `rho` field (`GpuState::rho`, the converged ρ_out — not the display texture, so it works with `set_display(false)` and reads the solver's own layout): one thread per point, 8 buffer reads, trilinear weights in f32, written to an output buffer and read back **once** (33 floats per candidate pair in round 1, 4 per survivor in round 2, a few more for a march in round 3).
- `SurrogateKs`: delegates to its *stepping* teacher only when the latest evaluation was a `Fallback` (the teacher's state is then this geometry's); otherwise `None`. `reset()` and `discontinuity()` clear that flag (S4: after a pass's solve through `teacher_mut` the teacher holds another geometry; the references never use the stepping teacher since the Task 3 review, I2). The background auditor's state is never sampled.
- `sim` stays ignorant of what produced the numbers: the detector consumes `Vec<f64>` and never sees a grid.

**The same operator (MUST-1 fix).** The detector builds a `Promolecule` from the references, the current positions and the scene's cell geometry, with two evaluators: `exact(p)` = Σ_C ρ̄_{Z_C}(|p − R_C|) from the tables, and `trilinear(p)` = Σ over the 8 grid nodes around p of w_node · exact(node) — the trilinear weights of `qm::Grid::trilinear`, i.e. the operator the provider applied to ρ. The **bias-corrected density** is ρ̂(p) = ρ(p) · exact(p) / trilinear(p) (ρ unchanged where trilinear(p) = 0, i.e. outside the grid — inside it the tables' tails continue their last log-slope, Task 3 review M4), and the deformation ratio q(p) = ρ̂(p)/exact(p) = ρ(p)/trilinear(p) is the review's same-operator ratio. Cost: 8 table lookups per sample point, nothing per grid point. The correction is exact for the promolecule itself and second order in the deformation for ρ (§3.1). The provider is asked for points in rounds: the chord samples, the transverse probes of the survivors, and then one small round per march step (7 samples per marching pair: the point and six probes) — a handful of readbacks of a few floats each.

### 3.4 When the detector runs; staleness under the surrogate

`Dynamics` runs the detector after every evaluation whose `sample_density` returns `Some` (every DFT step, every surrogate fallback) and every reference-cache change, on the worker thread, before the snapshot is published (two or three readbacks ≈ 0.5 ms; nothing on the render thread). The snapshot carries

```rust
pub enum Route { Saddle, Deformation }
pub enum Verdict { Bonded { multi_centre: bool }, TooFar, NoInteriorMinimum, NotATransverseMaximum, BelowFloor, NotAccumulated, NeighbourRegion { atom: usize } }
/// Every sampled pair, with the numbers behind its verdict (MUST-5): for the hover readout and the oracles.
pub struct PairReport { pub i: usize, pub j: usize, pub route: Option<Route>, pub s: Option<f64>, pub rho_min: Option<f64>, pub transverse_ratio: Option<f64>, pub rho_pro: Option<f64>, pub enhancement: Option<f64>, pub verdict: Verdict }
pub struct Bond { pub i: usize, pub j: usize, pub rho_bcp: f64, pub enhancement: f64, pub s: f64, pub route: Route, pub multi_centre: bool }   // the Bonded subset
pub struct BondGraph { pub bonds: Vec<Bond>, pub components: Vec<Vec<usize>>, pub step: u64, pub sim_time: f64, pub n_nuclei: usize }
// DynSnapshot:
pub bonds: Option<Arc<BondGraph>>,   // the latest graph (None before the first density or while references are pending)
pub bonds_stale: bool,               // true when the latest evaluation carried no density (a surrogate step) or the nucleus count changed since the graph
pub bonds_pending: Vec<u8>,          // species whose reference is still being solved
pub pair_reports: Vec<PairReport>,   // every pair within R_max, bonded or not
```

Under the surrogate the graph is stale for up to an audit interval, exactly as the density is (M5 handoff): the viewer draws the *topology* of the last DFT graph between the *current* nuclear positions, faded and labelled "bonds from the last DFT step (t = … fs) — the surrogate has no electrons", the same treatment as the faded cloud. A nucleus count change drops the graph until the next DFT evaluation (a stale graph with wrong indices is never drawn).

### 3.5 Molecules

A molecule is a connected component of the bond graph (union–find over the bonds; isolated nuclei are one-atom components). Components are listed with their formula and name (§4) next to the component's centroid in the 3D view and in the selection panel; a component with a multi-centre bond is labelled "ring" in the tooltip. The honest copy the user asked for goes with them: "bonds and names are read off the density after a converged DFT step" and "H₃ is not a stable molecule; H₃⁺ is".

### 3.6 Honest limits (stated in the UI copy and README)

- **Ionic and charged closed-shell bonds are the open question — LiH and HeH⁺ "may miss".** The accumulation test is a covalent criterion. In LiH the density at the bond point is small (ρ_b ≈ 0.04 in the literature) and the deformation density there combines H's gain with Li's loss of its diffuse 2s. In HeH⁺ the neutral promolecule (He's two electrons plus H's one, against the ion's two) along the axis 0.9–1.1 Bohr from He is 0.23–0.25 e/Bohr³ while the molecular density there is of order 0.15–0.2 (QTAIM classes HeH⁺ closed-shell-like): the predicted ratio is 0.6–0.9 and the detector is expected to say `NotAccumulated` (MUST-3). Both oracles are **reported, not asserted** (§11): the numbers are recorded as findings under rule 7, and a miss is never fixed by lowering δ (rule 6). **Measured (Task 7, `references.md`):** both come out `Bonded` on the σ = 0.25 surface — HeH⁺ by Route D (q 1.41; the smeared ion's density 0.27 against a neutral promolecule of 0.19), LiH by Route S (q 1.74, ρ_b 0.042 — the literature's ≈ 0.04). **No charge-dependent rescaling of the promolecule** (the N_e/ΣZ idea): it would mix a charge-redistribution model into what is meant to be a measurement — the honest statement is that a cation's bonds look weaker than they are under a neutral promolecule, and the readout says so. The candidate refinement, recorded so the investigation has a starting point: a second channel for closed-shell bonds using the sign of ∇²ρ at p* and the charge transfer visible in ρ along the chord.
- **Heavy atoms at h = 0.25** are smeared (M4 handoff): bond *topology* is expected right (the oracle asserts it for water), the X–H bond points come through Route D, and ρ_b values are not physical numbers — shown with that caveat.
- **Charged scenes** lower every ratio by the promolecule's extra electrons (H₃⁺ still clears 1 + δ with room: its edge-midpoint promolecule is 0.085 against ρ_b of order 0.1–0.13).
- The chord is a chord of the true bond path; the march is a line probe.

## 4. Formulas and names

### 4.1 The displayed formula and its charge

`formula(z, charge) -> String` (MUST-6): **carbon compounds in Hill order** (C, then H, then the rest alphabetically — Hill, J. Am. Chem. Soc. 22, 478 (1900): `CH₄`, `C₂H₆`, `CO₂`, `CHN`, `CH₄O`); **everything else in the IUPAC electronegativity-order sequence** of the Red Book 2005, IR-4.4.2.1 / Table VI, restricted to H–Ne and read from the electropositive end: **Ne, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, H, O, F** — so `H₂O`, `LiH`, `HF`, `NH₃`, `HeH⁺`, `H₃⁺`, `H₃O⁺`, `HO⁻` (the Red Book's own formula for hydroxide), `LiF`, `O₂`. This is a *display* rule (rule 1: the sequence is a table in `app`, never physics); the Hill string is kept as a function for the record (`hill_formula`) but is not what the label shows. ### 4.2 Structural classification from the bond graph

`classify(z, bonds, charge) -> Name` with, in this order of precedence:

1. **Trivial-name table hit** (§4.3) on the **composition** — sorted (Z, count) pairs — plus the charge and, where the table needs one, a graph signature (peroxide: an O–O bond with both O further bonded; methanol: a C–O bond). Never on a formula string, so a display-order change can never silently break a lookup (MUST-6).
2. **Homonuclear diatomic** X₂ → "di" + element name ("dihydrogen", "dioxygen"); X₃⁺ with a triangle is a table entry (trihydrogen cation).
3. **Peroxide**: an O–O bond where both O have at least one further bond → "{other part} peroxide" (hydrogen peroxide is the table hit; the structural rule covers e.g. a methyl peroxide seen later).
4. **Simple hydrocarbon**: every heavy atom is C and the carbons are mutually connected through C–C bonds; n_C ≤ 2 named by H count on the chain — n_H = 2n+2 "-ane", 2n "-ene", 2n−2 "-yne" with the meth/eth stem (methane, ethane, ethene, ethyne); n_C ≥ 3, rings or branches → "C_n hydrocarbon" (no isomer naming in M6).
5. **Binary compound** A_m B_n: A is the element earlier in the Table VI sequence above (Ne, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, H, O, F — the noble gases are the most electropositive end, which is what makes "helium hydride" come out right) — named "{prefix_m} A-name {prefix_n} B-stem-ide" with mono omitted on A and all prefixes omitted when A is hydrogen: "hydrogen oxide" (table → water), "lithium hydride", "boron trihydride" (table → borane), "carbon dioxide", "dinitrogen oxide", "helium hydride" (+ "cation" from the charge). Stems: hydr-, hel-, lith-, beryll-, bor-, carb-, nitr-, ox-, fluor-, neon-.
6. Anything else: formula only, `Structure::Unnamed`.

The result is `Name { formula: String, text: Option<String>, kind: Trivial | Structural | None }`; the UI marks structural names "(systematic)".

### 5.5 Validation: H₂ vs D₂

## 6. Relax tool

### 6.1 FIRE

FIRE (Fast Inertial Relaxation Engine; Bitzek, Koskinen, Gähler, Moseler & Gumbsch, PRL 97, 170201 (2006)) is velocity-Verlet MD with three feedback rules on the power P = F·v (3N-vector dot product):

- after each step, mix the velocity toward the force direction: v ← (1 − α) v + α |v| F̂;
- if P > 0 and more than N_min steps have passed since the last P ≤ 0: Δt ← min(f_inc Δt, Δt_max), α ← f_α α;
- if P ≤ 0: **backtrack half the last displacement, r ← ½(r_before + r_verlet)** (Guénolé et al., Comput. Mater. Sci. 175, 109069 (2020), the "uphill correction"; with velocity Verlet the step's displacement is Δt·v_half, so this is r −= ½Δt·v_half, not ½Δt·v_old — MUST-7), v ← 0, Δt ← max(f_dec Δt, Δt_min), α ← α_start.

Parameters (the paper's, which it shows are system-independent): N_min = 5, f_inc = 1.1, f_dec = 0.5, α_start = 0.1, f_α = 0.99. Δt starts at the run's dt (0.25 fs) and Δt_max = min(10 dt, 1.0 fs): Bitzek's 10 × Δt_MD would be 2.5 fs, but Verlet is stable only for ω Δt < 2, and a compressed H–X bond on this surface can reach ω ≈ 2 × ω_H₂(σ = 0.25) = 2.9e-2 a.u., so Δt_max = 1.0 fs = 41 a.u. keeps ω_max Δt ≈ 1.2 — FIRE's P ≤ 0 rule would catch an unstable step by halving Δt, but a kick that sends a nucleus into the box wall is not worth catching. Δt_min = 0.02 Δt_max (FIRE 2.0), so a P that flips sign near the egg-box floor cannot shrink Δt indefinitely before the stall detector speaks (S1). Pinned nuclei have F = 0 and v = 0 in the FIRE vectors (the integrator's pins apply as in MD); the COM correction and the drag spring stay in `augment` (relaxing while holding a nucleus on the spring is a legitimate use), and **the stop criterion uses the augmented force** — max_i |F_i^aug| < F_TOL — while the readout shows the raw provider max|F| (S1: a held nucleus relaxes against the spring and must be able to converge). The thermostat is bypassed during FIRE steps.

Chosen over damped Verlet (the validation suite's `relax` helper, which quenches v when P < 0) for its adaptive Δt and α, the method of record for many-mode relaxations (Bitzek 2006); the helper stays as the independent reference the relax oracle compares against (§11), which compares **energies**, not evaluation counts. Measured ratios of evaluations, FIRE/quench: on the analytic fixtures of Task 6 the quench needed 1.15–1.63× FIRE's; on H₃⁺ kicked 0.2 Bohr (Task 7) FIRE needed 1.56× the 0.5 fs quench's (25 vs 16) and 0.86× the 0.25 fs quench's (25 vs 29) — on a stiff few-atom problem FIRE spends its first N_min-limited steps growing Δt from the MD dt, so no speed-up claim is made for small molecules.

### 6.2 In the driver

`DynCommand::Relax` starts, `DynCommand::StopRelax` (and `Pause`) ends. A relax is a **tool mode** like a sampling pass: it runs in the driver's budgeted ticks (as many FIRE steps as fit, at least one), Play and Step are refused while it runs, edits are accepted (stale forces re-evaluated; FIRE restarts its Δt and α after an edit). **Bookkeeping (why it is a tool, MUST-7):** a FIRE step is not dynamics, so *everything* it changes is tool work — **W += ΔE_KS + ΔK for every FIRE step** (the Verlet displacement, the backtrack's position change, the mixing and the quench together: E(r_back) comes from the evaluation the next step needs anyway, E(r_verlet) is already known), so E_conserved = E_KS + K + U_tools − W is **constant to rounding** throughout a relax (the tool potentials U_spring, U_field, U_wall are booked with the step) and "work done by tools" shows exactly the energy the relaxation drained: ΔW = E_end − E_start once the velocities are zero — **negative**, since W is the driver's "energy put in" (the thermostat's convention, W += ΔK). `sim_time` does **not** advance (FIRE steps are not time), the step counter does not move, and the drift history restarts when the relax ends (the drift number is for dynamics; a flat relax segment inside its window would only flatter it). Stop criterion: max_i |F_i^aug| < F_TOL = 1e-3 Ha/Bohr (the brief; |F_i| is the per-atom vector norm, the same definition the validation suite's quench helper uses), well above the egg-box ripple (ΣF ≈ 3e-4 at h = 0.25; 2.2e-6 rms per component at 49³) so convergence is reachable; cap 2000 FIRE steps; stall detector: no 1 % improvement of max|F| over 200 steps ends the relax with a note. On completion velocities are zero and the snapshot's `relax` reads `converged: true/false`, steps, final max|F| (raw and augmented), E.

**On the surrogate:** the relax runs on the model (fast); at the end, one teacher solve at the relaxed geometry (`Trainable::force_teacher_next` then `evaluate`) is kept as a sample and reported as `verified_max_force`; if it exceeds F_TOL the readout says "relaxed on the model; DFT max|F| = …" and the Relax button offers to continue (a second Relax resumes from there, now with fresh samples refitted).

## 8. Input-replay harness

The M2 checklist (`docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-13-m2-viewer-checklist.md`) has grown three sections of "code reading only — re-check by hand"; posted OS mouse input never reached egui. M6 replaces the hand checks with recorded sessions replayed headlessly.

### 8.1 Event model

`app::replay::Event` is device-independent: `Cursor { x, y }`, `Button { which: Left | Right | Middle, down: bool }`, `Wheel { lines }`, `Key { key: Space | Delete, down }`, `Resize { w, h }`, `CursorLeft`, `FocusLost`. The window's `App` converts winit events to these and feeds **the same** `Viewer::input(&Event)` the replayer feeds — the tool logic moves out of `main.rs` into `viewer::input` so there is one code path; picks inside `Viewer::input` use the snapshot mirror `self.scene`, never the interpolated `shown_scene` (S5). Panel widgets call `Viewer::apply(Action)` for the things a replay must drive (`Action::Play`, `Pause`, `Step`, `Tool(mode)`, `Element(z)`, `Isotope(a)`, `Backend(b)`, `Preset(name)`, `Relax`, `Pin(i, bool)`, `Speed(n)`, `Thermostat(Option<(T, τ)>)`, `Delete(i)`, `SmoothMotion(bool)`, …): the egui layout — which the redesign branch is changing — is not part of a replay.

### 8.2 File format

Text, one line per entry, `#` comments, like the scene file:

```
# atomic sandbox replay v1
view 1280 800
action smooth-motion false
t 120 cursor 640 400
t 130 button left down
wait ms 270
t 400 cursor 700 420
t 410 button left up
action tool select
action element 8
action play
wait steps 20
wait idle
expect nuclei 3
expect paused false
expect selected 1
expect dist 0 1 1.5 2.5
expect bonds 2
expect molecule 0 H2O
expect no-error
```

`t` is the recorded wall time (ms) — informational; the replayer applies events in order and waits only at `wait` lines: `wait idle` (until the worker's `Busy(false)` **with no relax running and no reference job pending**), `wait ms N` (wall), `wait steps N` / `wait fs X` (on the snapshot's step counter / sim time — the deterministic way to let dynamics advance, S5: at DFT speed 300 ms is 1–2 steps on one GPU and 6 on another). The recorder (`--record <path>` on the windowed app) writes every event with its time, emits `wait ms` for any gap longer than 50 ms between events (so a recorded 400 ms drag replays as a 400 ms drag, not a one-frame jump of the spring target), and `wait idle` whenever the worker went idle during a gap. Every script starts with `action smooth-motion false` so picks and expectations see snapshot positions. `expect` lines assert on the `Viewer` after the preceding waits; a failure names the line.

### 8.3 Running

`--replay <path> [--preset p] [--backend b] [--screenshot out.png]` runs headlessly through the screenshot harness's `Headless` + `Viewer`, exits non-zero on a failed expectation. `cargo test -p app --release` gains `replays_pass`, which runs every `crates/app/replays/*.replay` (GPU; skipped without an adapter). The checklist note's "re-check by hand" rows are replaced by the script names.

- **P1** Charge superscript only for a single-component scene (§4.1); multi-component scenes state the unassigned net charge.
- **P2** The surrogate provenance *records* the mass set but does not *gate* on it (§5.3): the BO surface is mass-independent.

- **P6** Bond detection reference solves run without pausing the dynamics (§3.2); the graph is absent until they finish. As amended by the Task 3 review (I2): they are side jobs on a dedicated solver instance on their own thread — the stepping provider is never reset, no MD step is cold on their account, and no command waits for them.
- **P7** Three-centre bonds are detected by the **multi-centre march** (§3.1, the review's option (i)) rather than by waiving the transverse test inside a closed triangle: no test is waived, the rule generalises to cages, and it stays a statement about ρ's gradient field (a rise that ends at or stalls on a non-nuclear point is a ring point; one that runs into a nucleus is a neighbour's hill). The march is a re-steered gradient ascent with the stationary-point stop f_g = 0.25 (re-review R-1): the pre-measurement gives, for the water-like density at 0°/45°/90°/random rotation, a march that reaches O (rejected) every time, and for the H₃⁺-like density at bump heights +8 % to +15 %, flat or tilted, a stop 1.1–1.2 Bohr from every nucleus (kept).
- **P8** A relax books **ΔE_KS + ΔK of every FIRE step** into W (§6.2), not only the velocity edits: E_conserved is constant to rounding during a relax, and "work done by tools" is the energy drained.
- **P9** Displayed formulas: Hill order for carbon compounds, the Red Book Table VI sequence (Ne, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, H, O, F) otherwise; the name table is keyed by composition (§4.1–4.3).
- **P10** `ReferenceKey` is (n, h, σ, Z) — one radial table per species and grid, shared by every provider; the energy's tolerance/provider travel with the entry for the surrogate's stricter use (§3.2).
