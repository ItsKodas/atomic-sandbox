Rule 1 (CLAUDE.md) is the crux of this document: **the only per-element inputs stay Z and the isotope mass.** A pseudopotential here is a *derived object* — this project's own all-electron atom, transformed by one element-independent recipe whose every constant is universal and listed in §4.9. Nothing is loaded from a pseudopotential library; nothing is tuned per element. Where the standard recipes of the literature use hand-chosen per-element radii, this design replaces them with a universal rule plus element-independent acceptance gates (§4.4, §4.8), and says so.

## 1. The problem, quantitatively

### 1.1 What the grid must resolve

The all-electron Kohn-Sham solver puts every electron of every atom on one uniform grid of spacing h (0.25 Bohr default). The 1s orbital of a nucleus of charge Z is hydrogenic, R_1s ∝ e^{−Z_eff r}, with the maximum of r²R² at r = 1/Z_eff and ⟨r⟩ = 3/(2Z_eff). For O (Z_eff ≈ 7.7) that is 0.13 Bohr; C 0.17, N 0.15, Ne 0.10. The density at the oxygen nucleus is ρ(0) ≈ 2Z_eff³/π ≈ 3 × 10² e/Bohr³ (against 1/π for H).

The grid cannot represent the cusp at all (`crates/qm/src/nuclear.rs`, "Why smear"): the 8th-order stencil of `stencil.rs` turns a cusp into O(1) noise, so each nucleus is deposited as a Gaussian of width σ for the electrons, and the grid must then resolve *that* Gaussian — σ ≥ h for the analytic erf potential, σ ≳ 2h for the Poisson route the GPU used before M3 Task 10 (both solvers now evaluate the analytic erf; §11) (`nuclear.rs`, "Resolution of σ by the grid": the stencil error on a feature of width σ falls as (h/σ)⁸ and is 4e-3 at σ = 1.2h, 1.7e-5 at 2.4h). The smearing costs ΔE ≈ 2πZρ(0)σ² + O(σ³) + O(σ⁴), and the validation suite removes it by extrapolating a σ ladder to σ → 0 (`scf::extrapolate_sigma`). That series is only asymptotic for Zσ ≲ 0.2: measured in `crates/validate/references.md` ("Known limitation: Li"), He at Zσ_max = 0.48 misses by 46 mHa at h = 0.15, Li at Zσ_max = 0.72 by 331 mHa, and the stated requirement for the 40 mHa class is **Z·h ≲ 0.2**.

The size of the smearing error itself follows from scaling (review M6, re-derived and re-computed here): for one electron in the bare smeared well, −½∇² − Z erf(r/(σ√2))/r, the substitution r → r/Z gives **E(Z, σ) = Z² E(1, Zσ)**, and a 1-D Numerov solve of the Z = 1 well (shooting, node count, h = 0.005, exact −0.5 reproduced to 1e-5) gives E(1, x) = −0.4856, −0.4429, −0.3751, −0.2841, −0.1930 at x = Zσ = 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2 (at x = 0.1 the shift 0.0144 is 0.72× the first-order 2x², the relaxation ratio `references.md` measured for H). For O the shift per 1s electron is therefore 3.65, 8.0, 13.8, 19.7 Ha at Zσ = 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2; two 1s electrons at today's Zσ = 2 give 39 Ha ≈ the measured 74.5 − 36.6 = 38 Ha — the "halving" is the two 1s electrons, and nothing else. ### 1.2 What Zσ = 2 does to oxygen (why O is "half" its energy and a singlet)

At σ = 0.25 the smeared potential of O is a well of depth V(0) = −Z√(2/π)/σ = −25.5 Ha that is *harmonic* out to d ≈ a = σ√2 = 0.35 Bohr: expanding erf(d/a)/d gives V(d) ≈ V(0) + ½k d² with k = 4Z/(3√π a³) = 136 Ha/Bohr² (the same expansion as `forces.rs`, "g′(d) and its d → 0 limit"). A 3-D oscillator of that stiffness has ω = √k = 11.7 Ha, a ground state at V(0) + 3ω/2 ≈ −8 Ha and width (1/ω)^{1/2} = 0.29 Bohr. That harmonic figure is an *upper bound* (the true well lies below its parabola: −erf(x)/x = −(2/√π a)(1 − x²/3 + x⁴/10 − …), and the x⁴ term is negative); the actual bare-smeared 1s eigenvalue at Zσ = 2 is Z²·E(1, 2) = 64 × (−0.193) = **−12.3 Ha against the bare point-nucleus −Z²/2 = −32 Ha** — ≈ 20 Ha too high per electron, like for like (both bare, unscreened; the screened AE eigenvalue −18.8 Ha is a different reference and is not compared here). The core is twice its true size (0.29 vs 0.13 Bohr), the 2s and 2p that must stay orthogonal to it are pushed outward, and the sp energetics that set bond angles are wrong. Measured consequences, all in `references.md` and `crates/sim/src/sampling.rs`:

- O atom: E ≈ −36.6 Ha (33³/57³, σ = h = 0.25) against the resolved LSDA ≈ −74.5 Ha; the **singlet lies 0.227 Ha below the triplet** (−36.565 vs −36.338 at 33³; at 57³ the triplet and quintet do not even converge cold) — Hund's rule does not emerge (`gpu_oxygen_reference_multiplicity`, ignored with the numbers).
- H₂O: the σ = 0.25 surface's minimum is **O–H 2.34 Bohr, 177.2°**, 0.160 Ha below the physical geometry the preset loads (`thermostat_h2o_preset_known_limitation`), so Play heats water past 10 000 K.
- H₂: R_e 1.79 Bohr and ω = 3237 cm⁻¹ at σ = 0.25 against 1.45 Bohr / ≈ 4200 (LSDA) / 4401 (experiment) — H is only at Zσ = 0.25, and still loses 25 % of its frequency to the smearing.
- Li: the σ → 0 oracle misses by 331 mHa and is `#[ignore]`d.
- The egg-box: Σ_I F_I,y = −3.4e-4 Ha/Bohr for H₂O (the Z = 8 Gaussian moving across the grid), vs ≤ 4e-5 for H.

### 1.3 Why finer uniform grids do not scale

Points ∝ h⁻³. The LOBPCG iteration count grows with the condition number of H, whose top is the kinetic maximum ∝ 1/h² (the Jacobi preconditioner removes little of it), so iterations ∝ 1/h and the solve ∝ h⁻⁴ at best; the Poisson multigrid is ∝ h⁻³. From the measured H₂O 57³ solve of 1.8 s (0.25 Bohr), the 40 mHa class (h ≈ 0.025, with extrapolation) costs hours per MD step and the un-extrapolated 1 Ha class more — and that is for the *atom*; the molecule needs the box. The surrogate (M5) removed the dynamics bottleneck but not the teaching one: every teacher solve is one of these. No interactive path runs through h ≲ 0.05.

The smearing is a numerical device for a feature the valence electrons never need: the 1s of O is 0.13 Bohr wide, chemically inert, and 18.8 Ha below the valence. Removing it from the grid problem — rather than resolving it — is the whole content of this milestone.

## 2. Options

### (a) Norm-conserving pseudopotentials generated in-house — **recommended**

Replace, for each species with a core, the −Z/r nucleus plus its core electrons by an ionic potential that (i) is smooth inside a core radius r_c, (ii) equals −Z_v/r outside, (iii) reproduces the all-electron valence eigenvalues and the valence wavefunctions beyond r_c exactly at the reference configuration, and (iv) is norm-conserving (Hamann, Schlüter & Chiang, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 43, 1494 (1979)), which makes the logarithmic derivative's energy dependence correct to first order and so makes the potential transferable to molecules. Two constructions:

- **Troullier–Martins** (TM; Troullier & Martins, *Phys. Rev. B* 43, 1993 (1991)): one pseudo-wavefunction per l, r^{l+1} e^{p(r)} with an even polynomial p of degree 12 inside r_c, fixed by norm conservation, C⁴ matching at r_c and zero curvature of the screened potential at the origin; the potential follows by inverting the radial equation. Simple, closed-form, designed for smoothness, and the classical choice for first-row elements on real-space grids (Chelikowsky, Troullier & Saad, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 72, 1240 (1994); Chelikowsky, Troullier, Wu & Saad, *Phys. Rev. B* 50, 11355 (1994) — the PARSEC lineage this solver already follows for its stencil).
- **ONCV** (Hamann, *Phys. Rev. B* 88, 085117 (2013)): two projectors per l, optimised in a spherical-Bessel basis for a residual-kinetic-energy criterion (after Rappe, Rabe, Kaxiras & Joannopoulos, *Phys. Rev. B* 41, 1227 (1990)), with norm conservation generalised to two reference energies. More accurate at a given smoothness and the only way to carry a semicore and a valence state of the same l; much heavier to implement (constrained optimisation, generalised eigenproblems).

Both are "Z in, potential out" once the recipe's choices are fixed universally (§4). The literature fixes r_c, the local channel and the partial-core radius per element by hand; this design derives them (§4.4) — the one place where rule 1 is not automatically satisfied by the method and must be enforced by design.

Trade-offs: a new 1-D solver and generator (CPU, seconds per element); a nonlocal operator in the Hamiltonian (cheap: §5.3); the frozen-core approximation (core–core overlap at short distances must be flagged, §5.6); the egg-box moves from the nuclear Gaussian to the projectors and short-range local part (reduced, not gone — §5.1, §7); ghost states (§4.6). Absolute energies become valence energies (§6.5 — labelled).

### (b) Projector-augmented waves (Blöchl, *Phys. Rev. B* 50, 17953 (1994))

Keeps the all-electron wavefunction reconstructible through partial waves and compensation charges; softer than NC potentials and the standard of GPAW/VASP. Heavier on every axis: partial-wave bases and overlap operator (a generalised eigenproblem in LOBPCG), compensation densities in the Poisson solve, one-centre corrections in every energy and force term, and a much larger generation apparatus. The same rule-1 questions (radii, partial-wave sets) recur. The accuracy gain over NC-TM is not needed for first-row chemistry at the honesty level this project reports. **Rejected for this milestone;** the `Ionic` abstraction of §5 does not preclude it.

### (c) Frozen-core all-electron on a finer local grid near nuclei (multi-resolution)

Keep the cusp (or a narrow σ) on nested finer grids around each nucleus (Ono & Hirose's double grid, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 82, 5016 (1999), extended to the whole core; adaptive coordinates per Gygi & Galli, *Phys. Rev. B* 52, R2229 (1995) or Modine, Zumbach & Kaxiras, *Phys. Rev. B* 55, 10289 (1997)). Honest and rule-1-trivial (no recipe at all), but every kernel — stencil, Poisson multigrid, density accumulation, LOBPCG's inner products, the renderer's volume — would have to handle non-uniform resolution and the interfaces between levels, the moving nuclei would drag the fine patches across the coarse grid (a worse egg-box), and the f32 GPU path would have to carry 10² e/Bohr³ next to 10⁻³. **Kept as a note:** if a later milestone wants all-electron heavy atoms for a specific reason (core-level spectroscopy, nuclear events needing the core density at the nucleus), this is the route, and the double-grid part of it is already this design's plan B for the projector integrals (§5.1).

### (d) Finer uniform grids with the smeared nucleus

Honest by construction (the existing solver, smaller h) and the right thing for the *oracles*. §1 prices it at 10²–10⁴× per solve for oxygen and more for Z > 10; not an interactive route and not a teaching route. Stays what it is: the validation reference for the all-electron limit (§6.1).

**Recommendation: (a), TM first, KB-separable, with NLCC, under a universal recipe and element-independent gates; ONCV as the stage-3 upgrade for semicore cases (§4.3).** Reasons: it is the smallest change to the real-space solver (one local field, a handful of localised projectors, three force terms), it is what every real-space DFT code of this family does, its generation is seconds on the CPU from the all-electron atom we must write anyway, and its approximations are all measurable against our own all-electron atom (rule 7).

## 3. Non-goals

- No pseudopotential files loaded from anywhere (rule 1). Tables are generated at run time (and cached, keyed by the recipe hash) — never shipped as data.
- No ultrasoft (Vanderbilt, *Phys. Rev. B* 41, 7892 (1990)) or PAW this milestone.
- No relativistic corrections (scalar-relativistic radial equations are a documented later extension for Z ≳ 30; irrelevant for the first two rows).
- No GGA: the generator uses the same LSDA-PW92 as the grid solver, and the XC identity is part of the recipe hash so a later PBE regenerates everything.
- **Future requirement (precision milestone, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-15-precision-design.md` §5.2.1, review M2):** a QMC recipe variant — a radial spin-restricted Hartree–Fock reference atom (`xc = HF`) with **`nlcc off`**, used in DMC in its semilocal form — must be expressible as a universal switch in `recipe_hash` and pass this spec's gates; this milestone's LSDA/NLCC potentials are not usable in QMC as they are (QMC has no ρ_pc).
- No change to the σ → 0 all-electron oracles: they test the solver and remain as they are.

## 4. The generation pipeline (rule-1-honest)

Everything in this section lives in a new module tree `crates/qm/src/radial/` (CPU, f64, no GPU), with the universal constants in one table (§4.9).

### 4.1 Radial all-electron LSDA atom (`qm::radial::atom`)

Spherical, spin-polarised Kohn-Sham atom on a logarithmic grid r_i = r_0 e^{i·δ} with universal r_0 = 1e-5 Bohr, r_max = 60 Bohr, δ = 0.008 (the grid rule is Z-independent; its usefulness for the 1s requires r_0 ≪ 1/Z, which 1e-5 satisfies for every Z ≤ 118). For each (n, l, σ) the radial equation −½u″ + [l(l+1)/2r² + V_σ(r)]u = ε u is integrated outward from r_0 and inward from r_max and matched at the outermost classical turning point by the shooting method (node counting fixes n; bisection on ε to 1e-13·max(1, |ε|) — equivalent in result to a Newton finish, measured 0.03–0.25 s per solve (Task 1 review S5)), in the transformed variable of the log grid with Numerov's rule — the standard atomic-structure method (Hartree, *The Calculation of Atomic Structures*, Wiley 1957; Herman & Skillman, *Atomic Structure Calculations*, Prentice-Hall 1963; Fuchs & Scheffler, *Comput. Phys. Commun.* 119, 67 (1999) for the modern pseudopotential-generator form of it).

- Potential: V_σ = −Z/r + V_H[ρ] + v_xc,σ[ρ↑, ρ↓]. The radial Hartree potential by the two cumulative integrals (Gauss's law); the XC exactly `qm::xc::lsda_pw92` on the radial densities (code reuse, not a re-derivation).
- Occupations: **derived, never tabulated.** The integer aufbau of the 3-D solver (`qm::scf`, "Electron count and multiplicity") applied radially: each SCF step occupies the lowest eigenvalues, with the spherical average over m (fractional occupation per m of an open shell), and the multiplicity found by the S9 scan (solve the candidates, keep the lowest energy — `crates/sim/src/sampling.rs`). Near-degenerate shells (4s/3d and beyond) can make the aufbau order oscillate between SCF steps; the universal remedy is the same scan: every occupation pattern that differs from the aufbau one by moving electrons between shells within Δ_scan = 0.1 Ha of the highest occupied level is solved, and the lowest total energy wins. No ground-state configuration table exists anywhere in the code (rule 1: a configuration table would be per-element data). For Z ≤ 18 the aufbau order is unambiguous and the scan is a no-op.
- Mixing: DIIS on the radial densities (the 3-D code's `diis` logic on 1-D vectors), tol 1e-10 on ∫|Δρ| r² dr.
- Spin-restricted mode: the same code with ρ↑ = ρ↓ (needed by §4.3).
- Outputs: ε_nlσ, u_nlσ(r), ρ_σ(r), the total energy with its components, and the per-shell outermost maximum r_max,nl of |u| and outermost node r_node,nl.

First tests (the per-element *references* here are test oracles, not inputs — same status as the NIST numbers already in `references.md`): H −0.478671, He −2.834836, Li −7.343957 Ha (NIST LSD column, Kotochigova et al. 1997; the −7.335195 previously quoted here and in `references.md` is that table's LDA column — corrected by the Task 1 review) to 1e-5; C, N, O, Ne LSD totals from the same table (read off at task time; the spec does not quote digits it has not checked against the source) to 1e-4. These comparisons are like for like: the NIST LSD column is computed with VWN5 correlation (the plan review measured VWN5 reproducing H and He to ~1e-6, while PW92 misses the 1e-5 gate by 4× for H and 38× for He), so the oracles run the radial solver with a test-only VWN5 and NIST's spin interpolation, and the generator's PW92 totals are reported against NIST (§11). The other first tests: the Hund triplet of O lowest among M = 1, 3, 5 in the spherical LSDA atom; the hydrogenic check of the integrator itself (V_H and v_xc off: **|ε_nl − (−Z²/2n²)| < 1e-10·Z²** for several n, l, Z — Numerov's eigenvalue error is O(δ⁴) relative and the log grid is scale-free, so the absolute error grows as Z²; plus the order check: the Z = 1 1s error ratio between δ = 0.016 and 0.008 in [14, 18], §11); and the **LSDA scaling identity** for every converged atom, to 1e-5 relative to T_s. The textbook virial −V/T = 2 is false for PW92 and would fail by ~10⁻³–10⁻² (review M2); re-derived: under ψ_λ(r) = λ^{3/2}ψ(λr), ρ_λ(r) = λ³ρ(λr), one has T_s → λ²T_s, E_en → λE_en, E_H → λE_H, while E_xc^{LDA}[ρ_λ] = ∫ρ(r′) ε_xc(λ³ρ↑(r′), λ³ρ↓(r′)) d³r′ has λ-derivative at 1 equal to 3∫ρ Σ_σ ρ_σ ∂ε_xc/∂ρ_σ = 3[Σ_σ∫ρ_σ v_xc,σ − E_xc] (with v_xc,σ = ε_xc + ρ ∂ε_xc/∂ρ_σ). Stationarity of the total energy at λ = 1 gives
  **2T_s + E_en + E_H + 3[Σ_σ ∫ρ_σ v_xc,σ d³r − E_xc] = 0.**
For Dirac exchange ρ ∂ε_x/∂ρ = ε_x/3, so the exchange part of the bracket collapses to E_x and the textbook 2T + V = 0 is recovered; for PW92 correlation it does not (the high-density ε_c ≈ A ln r_s has ρ dε_c/dρ = −A/3, a per-electron ≈ −0.01 Ha that leaves −V/T ≈ 2 off at the 10⁻³ level for O and ~1 % for H). The generalised identity is a stronger check of V_H, v_xc and the eigensolver than the textbook one, and is the one tested.

### 4.2 Core / valence by a universal rule (`qm::radial::partition`)

1. Seed: every occupied shell with the highest occupied principal quantum number n_max is valence.
2. Promote whole lower shells: the shell n = n_max − 1 (all its subshells) is promoted to valence — "semicore" — if any of its occupied subshells has ε_nl > ε_v,min − Δ_core, with ε_v,min the lowest seed eigenvalue and **Δ_core = 2.0 Ha** (universal). Repeat for n_max − 2 (never triggers below the f block). Everything else is core.
3. Channels: a pseudopotential channel is generated for every l ≤ l_max, l_max = max(l occupied in the valence) + 1, provided a bound state of that l exists in the spin-restricted reference potential. An unbound channel is dropped and recorded in `dropped`; its scattering is left to the local part (§4.4). For an unoccupied channel, r_c,l is **capped at max_{l occupied} r_c,l** (review S5), so a diffuse, barely bound state cannot set a projector sphere larger than the occupied channels'. Where the cap binds (measured, Task 2): Be, whose unoccupied 2p (ε = −0.077 Ha, r_max 2.28 Bohr) takes r_c,s at every f. Its r_c can then lie inside its own r_max (2.04 < 2.28 at f = 1.0), which is harmless for a nodeless state (§4.4). The neutral LSDA atom's potential has no −1/r tail (self-interaction), so it has no Rydberg series. The 3d is unbound for B–Ne *and* for Al–Ar (measured, Task 2). The earlier "3d of Al–Ar at ε ≈ −0.02 Ha" was the −1/r-tail estimate and does not occur. B–Ne and Al–Ar therefore have s and p channels only: one s projector, p local (§4.4 item 1). For Al–Ar this means the d-wave scattering is the p-local potential's. It is not gated (T5 excludes an ungenerated l). G2's count does check that this potential binds no spurious d state, and the d log-derivative mismatch is reported (Task 12). It is a stated limitation until the ONCV stage, which can build an unbound channel from a scattering state.
   Scope note for Z > 18 (review S9, not this milestone): "promote the whole n_max − 1 shell" would promote 3s, 3p and 3d together for Sc–Zn and Ga–Kr (3d within 2 Ha of 4s) and refuse the whole d block through a 3s/4s two-s-state refusal; the subshell-wise window (promote only the subshells inside Δ_core) is equally universal and is what practice does (3d semicore only). The rule is revisited with the ONCV stage before the d block is enabled.
4. Consequences (derived, not chosen): H and He have no core → no pseudopotential under the default policy (§4.7 — they stay all-electron smeared, identical to today); under `All` the no-core branch is explicit in the partition/NLCC code (ρ_c ≡ 0, N_c = 0, r_nlc undefined, ρ_pc = 0, r_core = 0 in §5.6). Li: 1s (−1.9 Ha) is within 2 Ha of 2s (−0.1) → the whole n = 1 shell is valence → the s channel carries two valence states, which a one-projector TM/KB potential cannot represent (§4.3) → the generator **refuses** Li (reported, not patched) and Li remains the all-electron known limitation until ONCV. Be–Ne: 1s core (Be 1s ≈ −3.9 Ha vs 2s −0.2: gap 3.7). Na, Mg: the n = 2 shell promoted (Na 2p ≈ −1.0 Ha vs 3s −0.1) → 9/10-electron semicore potentials, which again need two s-projectors → ONCV stage. Al–Ar: n = 2 core (Al 2p ≈ −2.6 Ha vs 3p −0.1). These eigenvalues are quoted here only to show what the rule does; the code reads them from its own atom.

### 4.3 Construction per l (`qm::radial::tm`)

Reference configuration: the **spin-restricted ground configuration** (ρ↑ = ρ↓) found in §4.1. The ionic potential must be spin-independent — it stands in for the nucleus plus a closed core — and the standard that makes a spin-restricted-generated potential valid in spin-polarised calculations is the nonlinear core correction (§4.5; Louie, Froyen & Cohen, *Phys. Rev. B* 26, 1738 (1982) introduced the NLCC for exactly this purpose). The spin-polarised atom of §4.1 is the *gate* (§4.8), not the generation input. (The brief's "spin-polarised, lowest-energy multiplicity" is therefore where the radial solver is validated and where the transferability is tested; it is not what the potential is built from. This is a deliberate departure, with the reason stated.)

TM (PRB 43, 1993, eqs. 4–13), for each channel l with reference eigenvalue ε_l and AE radial function u_l^{AE}:
- u_l^{PP}(r) = u_l^{AE}(r) for r ≥ r_c,l; u_l^{PP}(r) = r^{l+1} exp(p(r)), p = c₀ + c₂r² + c₄r⁴ + … + c₁₂r¹², for r < r_c,l, the seven coefficients fixed by: norm conservation ∫₀^{r_c}|u^{PP}|² = ∫₀^{r_c}|u^{AE}|²; continuity of u and its first four derivatives at r_c; and zero curvature of the screened potential at the origin. Derivation of that condition (review M1): with u = r^{l+1}e^{p}, u″/u = l(l+1)/r² + 2(l+1)p′/r + p′² + p″, so inverting the radial equation gives V_scr = ε_l + (l+1)p′/r + ½(p′² + p″); inserting p′/r = 2c₂ + 4c₄r² + …, p′² = 4c₂²r² + …, p″ = 2c₂ + 12c₄r² + … yields **V_scr(r) = ε_l + (2l+3)c₂ + 2[c₂² + (2l+5)c₄] r² + O(r⁴)**, hence V_scr″(0) = 0 ⇔ **c₂² + (2l+5)c₄ = 0** (TM eq. 12) and the analytic origin value **V_scr(0) = ε_l + (2l+3)c₂**. Solved by Newton on c₂ with the linear 5×5 system for the rest at each step, exactly as TM describe.
- V_l^{scr}(r) = ε_l − l(l+1)/(2r²) + u″/(2u) by inversion (nodeless by construction, so the division is safe; at r → 0 the closed form above is used).
- Unscreening: V_l^{ion} = V_l^{scr} − V_H[ρ_v^{PP}] − v_xc[ρ_v^{PP} + ρ_pc] with ρ_v^{PP} the pseudo-valence density of the reference configuration and ρ_pc the partial core of §4.5 (the unscreening must use the same partial core the molecule will use, or the atom is not reproduced).
- Beyond max(max_l r_c,l, r_nlc) every channel's ionic potential is the nucleus screened by the frozen core: V_l^ion = −Z/r + V_H[ρ_c] = −Z_v/r + τ_c(r), with the core tail τ_c(r) = V_H[ρ_c](r) − N_c/r = −∫_r^∞ 4πr′²ρ_c(r′)(1/r − 1/r′)dr′ ≤ 0. This uses the full ρ_c, not ρ_pc: the partial core never enters V_H. τ_c is physics, independent of f, and is kept, never "extended" away. The generator verifies this identity and computes r_cmp, the smallest radius from which |V_l^ion + Z_v/r| < `v_ion_tail` (1e-8) for every channel. r_cmp is the radius where τ_c itself falls below 1e-8 whenever that lies beyond max r_c; otherwise it lies no further out than the first grid point beyond max(r_c, r_nlc). Measured (Task 2): O 1.53 Bohr at every f (τ_c = −4.5e-8 at 1.41), Be 3.21, Al 4.18, Ar 2.45. r_cmp is what §5.1 uses (R_SR = max(r_cmp, r_floor)); it is not a core radius (§5.6 uses r_core).

Limitations stated: one reference state per l (the lowest valence state of that l). A channel with two valence states of the same l (Li, Na, Mg — §4.2) is **refused**, with the message naming the ONCV stage; the recipe never silently pseudises the lower one and hopes.

### 4.4 Cutoff radii and the local channel by a universal rule (`qm::radial::recipe`)

Per channel, r_c,l = f · r_max,l where r_max,l is the outermost maximum of |u_l^{AE}| (beyond the outermost node, so the pseudo-wavefunction is nodeless — the TM requirement; a capped unoccupied channel (§4.2 item 3) may have r_c inside its own r_max, as Be's 2p does, which is allowed for a nodeless state; `tm_channel` refuses any r_c inside a node). f is **not per element**: it is taken from the universal ladder **F = {1.6, 1.4, 1.2, 1.0}**, softest first, and the first f for which the potential passes every gate of §4.6 and §4.8 is kept. If none passes, the element is refused at this recipe version (reported in the generation record; never worked around by hand). The classical hand-chosen TM radii for C/N/O (≈ 1.3–1.5 Bohr, PRB 43, 1993, Table I) correspond to f ≈ 1.2 (C: 1.5/1.23), ≈ 1.45 (N), ≈ 1.6 (O: 1.45/0.9) on these elements' 2p maxima — a spread the ladder covers, which is the point: the rule lands where the literature did, as a check, not an input. The ladder's *outputs* are made visible (review S6a): a pinned table in `references.md` of (Z → f, local channel, r_c,l, Z_v, E_l^{KB}, GSS energy, h_req) for every accepted element, regenerated and diffed by the validation task, so any change to a universal constant that moves one element's f shows in review — the mechanical form of §9's "discipline" guard.

Molecular gate on f where an all-electron reference is affordable (review S6b): a pseudo-H at f = 1.6 (r_c = 1.694 > R_e = 1.45; the LSDA 1s r_max is 1.059 Bohr, Task 3) passes every *atomic* gate, because T5 at r_d = 2.03 Bohr lies outside the bond — and the ladder would have no reason to descend if §6.4's H₂ test failed. So for any element whose all-electron σ → 0 reference is affordable at the 40 mHa class (Z ≤ 2 today), the **homonuclear dimer against the element's own all-electron σ → 0 curve** (R_e within 0.02 Bohr, ω within 5 % — the §6.4 numbers) is an additional gate on f, applied in the same descending order. It is Z-derived (the dimer of the element), not per-element data. The gate is a 3-D computation the radial generator cannot run, so it is supplied by the providers. A table for such an element generated *without* it is **provisional**: it is reported, and no ionic setup may use it, so the ungated and gated potentials of one element can never coexist under one `recipe_hash` (§4.9, §11). The bracket in which the dimer's minimum is sought, [1, 4]·r_peak of the outermost valence shell, is physical: covalent R_e lies at ≈ 1.5–3 r_peak (H₂: 1.45 Bohr), while LSDA's spurious rare-gas minima lie beyond 4 r_peak. An element whose AE dimer has no minimum there (He) is "not applicable", and its record carries the PP and AE bond forces at both bracket ends.

Local channel: the universal preference order is
1. V_loc = V_{l_max}^{ion} (the highest generated channel: for the first row, with no bound d state, that is the p channel — the classic p-local O/N/C potentials);
2. a smooth polynomial local (ONCV §II.C-style): V_loc(r) = −Z_v/r + τ_c(r) for r ≥ r_loc (τ_c the core's Hartree tail, §4.3; 0 without a core), continued inside by the even polynomial matched C² at r_loc, with r_loc = min_l r_c,l.
The first candidate that passes the ghost test (§4.6) at the current f is used; the remaining channels become projectors. Both candidates are smooth and finite at the origin.

Semicore channels, l_max, Δ_core, F, the order of local candidates, the NLCC crossover (§4.5) and the gate thresholds (§4.8) are the complete list of recipe choices; §4.9 tabulates them with their justification.

### 4.5 Nonlinear core correction (`qm::radial::nlcc`)

LSDA is nonlinear in the density, so E_xc[ρ_v] ≠ E_xc[ρ_v + ρ_c] − E_xc[ρ_c], and the error is largest exactly where it hurts a spin-polarised calculation (the open-shell O triplet). Louie, Froyen & Cohen (PRB 26, 1738 (1982)) keep a *partial* core density in the XC functional: ρ_pc(r) = ρ_c(r) for r ≥ r_nlc, A sin(Br)/r for r < r_nlc with A, B fixed by value and slope continuity at r_nlc. Universal rule for r_nlc: the radius where **ρ_c(r) = ρ_v(r)** (LFC's own criterion, factor 1; the crossover is a property of the atom, not a number entered per element), taken as the outermost radius with ρ_c ≥ ρ_v and with ρ_v the *all-electron* atom's valence density, which does not depend on f. In the molecule, E_xc is evaluated on ρ_v,σ + ½ρ_pc per spin (the core is spin-unpolarised); ρ_pc never enters the Hartree term, the electron count, or the displayed valence density (it is a labelled option in the renderer, §6.5). It must be smooth enough for the grid, which the Fourier filter of §5.1 guarantees.

### 4.6 Kleinman–Bylander form and the ghost-state gate (`qm::radial::kb`)

The semilocal potential V_loc + Σ_l |δV_l P_l| (δV_l = V_l^{ion} − V_loc, P_l the angular projector) is made separable (Kleinman & Bylander, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 48, 1425 (1982)):

  V_NL = Σ_{lm} |δV_l φ_lm⟩⟨δV_l φ_lm| / ⟨φ_l|δV_l|φ_l⟩,  φ_lm = u_l^{PP}(r)/r · Y_lm(r̂) (orthonormal real harmonics),

i.e. a projector β_lm = δV_l φ_lm with the KB energy E_l^{KB} = 1/⟨φ_l|δV_l|φ_l⟩ (the radial integral ∫u_l² δV_l dr, Y_lm orthonormal). The operator is invariant to the scale of φ only if numerator and denominator use the same function, so the grid-side normalisation is pinned here (review S1): `qm::poisson` provides R_lm = r^l S_lm with Racah-normalised S_lm (∫|S_lm|² dΩ = 4π/(2l+1)), hence the grid function is
  **β_lm(r) = δV_l(r) · [u_l^{PP}(r)/r^{l+1}] · √((2l+1)/4π) · R_lm(r − R_I)**
(the r^{−(l+1)} table is finite at the origin since u ∝ r^{l+1}); using R_lm unscaled would multiply V_NL by 4π/(2l+1). The generation record reports, next to E_l^{KB}, the GSS energy ⟨φ|δV²|φ⟩/⟨φ|δV|φ⟩ (same sign — the quantity literature values mean). Every δV_l vanishes analytically beyond r_β = max(max_l r_c,l, r_nlc), where each V_l^ion is −Z_v/r + τ_c (§4.3): bitwise for the highest-channel local, and for the polynomial local up to the identity's quadrature residual (≈ 1e-12 Ha, a ΔQ/r tail from the Hartree cumulative's step across r_c, common to all channels). The generator sets β_l to zero beyond r_β after checking that residual against `v_ion_tail`/100, and r_β is the projector's support for the filter (§5.1) and the sphere (§5.3). A 1e-12-relative rule would read the residual instead (Task 3, polynomial local: Al p 5.28 Bohr against r_β = 4.25). By construction V_NL reproduces the semilocal operator on the reference state of each l; on other states it is an approximation that can produce **ghost states** — spurious eigenvalues below or near the valence ones. Gonze, Stumpf & Scheffler (*Phys. Rev. B* 44, 8503 (1991)) give the criterion: with ε₀^{loc} < ε₁^{loc} the two lowest eigenvalues of the local-only radial Hamiltonian of channel l (V_loc + screening), and ε_l the reference eigenvalue: no ghost iff (E_l^{KB} < 0 and ε_l < ε₀^{loc}) or (E_l^{KB} > 0 and ε₀^{loc} < ε_l < ε₁^{loc}). The variational principle restricts which failures can occur. With H_sl φ = ε_s φ and H_sl = H_loc + δV, ⟨φ|H_loc|φ⟩ = ε_s − ⟨φ|δV|φ⟩ ≥ ε₀^{loc}. So ε₀^{loc} > ε_s forces E_KB < 0, which GSS calls ghost-free, and E_KB > 0 forces ε₀^{loc} < ε_s. The two real ghost cases are therefore **(A) E_KB > 0 with ε₁^{loc} < ε_s** and **(B) E_KB < 0 with ε₀^{loc} < ε_s** (§11). Two gates, both element-independent:

- G1 (GSS criterion): must hold for every projector channel.
- G2 (direct): the self-consistent radial KB atom at the reference configuration reproduces the reference valence eigenvalues to **< 1 mHa**, and in every channel l ≤ l_max has **no bound state beyond the all-electron atom's own** below top = min(0, ε_v,max + 0.5 Ha). The exact count n_KB(top) must not exceed n_AE,l(top), the number of the spin-restricted AE reference's bound states of that l above its core below top (`Partition::spectrum`). "Extra" is measured against the AE atom, not against the generated channels. The neutral LSDA potential has no −1/r tail, hence no Rydberg series, but for l = 0 (no centrifugal barrier) its short-range well binds one weak (n+1)s for every element Be–Ar (measured, Task 3: C 3s −0.0061, O 3s −0.0045, Ne 3s −0.0024, Al 4s −0.0121, Si 4s −0.0138 Ha; unchanged to ≤ 3e-5 Ha with the grid extended from 60 to 480 Bohr). The KB potentials reproduce it (worst 6.4e-4 Ha, Ar). It is an LSDA artefact, not the real atom's Rydberg level, and is reported, never gated on its energy. A count *below* n_AE,l (an AE state the pseudo-atom does not bind) is not a ghost: it is recorded in the generation record and judged by T5, whose window reaches ε_v,max + 1 Ha > 0. A count at the continuum edge has no energy tolerance. A dropped l (no bound AE state) must hold none. The radial KB equation is integro-differential, −½u″ + [l(l+1)/2r² + V_loc + V_scr]u + E_l^{KB} β_l(r)⟨β_l|u⟩ = εu. Solve the homogeneous equation and the β_l-sourced particular equation, then impose the self-consistency c = E_l^{KB}⟨β_l|u⟩: the eigenvalue condition is a 2×2 determinant M in (c, the matching mismatch). It is evaluated in its Green-function form E_KB·W·s(E) = −W + 4E_KB∫β u_i C_h dr, with C_h(r) = ∫₀^r β u_h and G_loc(r, r′) = −(2/W)u_h(r<)u_i(r>). That form equals det M/r_m but has no cancellation between growing solutions at deep energies (Task 3). Node counting does not order a nonlocal operator's eigenvalues. The count of states below E is instead the **exact inertia count**: by Haynsworth's inertia additivity, n_KB(E) = n_loc(E) + [s(E) < 0] − [E_KB > 0], with s(E) = −1/E_KB − ⟨β|G_loc(E)|β⟩ = det M/(E_KB·W(u_h, u_i)), and n_loc(E) the local channel's node count. "Exact" holds up to the two discretisations of each local eigenvalue (node jump, W's zero), which differ by ≤ 2e-12 Ha (measured). A mesh locates the roots for the eigenvalue comparison, and its root count must equal the inertia count (§11). Task 3 implements exactly that. (Task 3 round 1: n_loc is the Sturm count of the Dirichlet problem on [0, r_ie], so u_i starts from u_i(r_ie) = 0; the WKB-ratio start of the AE shooting is not that solution once a level's decay length exceeds the box, and the count then fell from 2 to 1 just below E = 0 — the polynomial-local Al at f = 1.0 read as missing its 4s, which it binds at −0.01219 Ha against AE −0.01210.)

G1 is the fast check applied while scanning f and the local candidates; G2 is the proof. A failure moves to the next local candidate, then to the next f (§4.4).

### 4.7 Policy: which species get pseudopotentials

`IonicPolicy` is a scene-wide (never per-species) setting:
- `CoresOnly` (default at first): a species is pseudised iff §4.2 gives it a core; H, He (and any refused element) stay all-electron Gaussian-smeared at σ = h as today.
- `All`: every species is pseudised by the same recipe, including nodeless 1s valence (H: r_c = f · r_max,1s with the LSDA r_max = 1.059 Bohr → r_c = 1.694 Bohr at f = 1.6 — the literature's usual pseudo-H range of 1.0–1.25 Bohr sits at f ≈ 1.0–1.25, inside the ladder). This removes σ from the whole system: H₂'s R_e and ω become grid-converged quantities instead of σ-extrapolated ones (§6.4). It is the intended default once its gates pass (§8, Task 11); until then it is a labelled switch.
- `None`: today's all-electron solver (the oracles, the gpu_vs_cpu suite, and the fallback for any species the recipe refuses).

The policy is global, so no per-element decision is ever entered; which species actually carry a potential is an *output* of §4.2.

### 4.8 Transferability gates (element-independent acceptance; rule 6)

Run on every generated potential, in the radial code, before it may be used on the grid. Thresholds are universal; a miss is a refused potential (then the next f), never a loosened threshold.

|---|---|---|

| T2 norm | ∫₀^{r_c} u² equal AE vs PP (TM's construction, as a regression) | < 1e-10 |
| T3 excitation / transfer energies | AE vs KB-PP total-energy *differences* between the reference configuration and each of: the +1 ion; the s → p promotion (one electron from the highest valence s to the lowest valence p, when both exist); the −1 anion if bound in both; the spin-polarised Hund ground multiplet (the §4.1 scan); re-run with the filtered tables at the scene's h (S2) | each ΔE within **5 mHa** |

| T5 log derivatives, gated in energy units | D_l(E) = u′/u at r_d = 1.2 · max_l r_c,l, AE vs KB-PP, E ∈ [ε_v,min − 1 Ha, ε_v,max + 1 Ha] on a 10 mHa mesh, for the projector channels and the local channel (not an ungenerated l, where the local potential is not the AE scattering by design); the mismatch converted to an equivalent eigenvalue error, computed in the pole-free Wronskian form **δE_equiv(E) = ½·W(û_AE, û_PP)(r_d) = ½[û_AE û′_PP − û′_AE û_PP](r_d)**, each regular solution normalised to ∫₀^{r_d} û² dr = 1 (derivation below; §11) | max_E |δE_equiv| < **20 mHa** |

| T7 ghost | G1, G2 of §4.6; G2's count also in the KB atom of every T3 configuration (per spin), against that configuration's AE atom's bound spectrum above the core | as stated |
| T8 resolution requirement (derived output, in energy units — review S2) | with the tables filtered for each h on the ladder {0.35, 0.30, 0.25, 0.20, 0.15} (§5.1), the radial KB atom is re-solved; **h_req = the coarsest h at which the filtered atom reproduces the unfiltered total energy and every valence eigenvalue within 1 mHa** | recorded per element; the app warns when the scene's h > h_req |

T1–T4 compare our pseudo-atom with our all-electron atom — the honest reference (rule 7). T1 and T3 thresholds are the brief's.

Why T5 is gated in energy, not radians (review S4, re-derived): for two solutions u_E, u_E′ of −½u″ + (V_l − E)u = 0, both regular at the origin, d/dr[u_E u_E′′ − u_E′ u_E′] = 2(E − E′)u_E u_E′; integrating to r_d and dividing by u_E(r_d)u_E′(r_d) gives D_E′ − D_E = 2(E − E′)∫₀^{r_d}u_E u_E′ dr/(u_E u_E′)(r_d), so **∂D/∂E = −(2/u(r_d)²)∫₀^{r_d}u² dr** — the norm-conservation identity (equal norms inside r_d ⇒ equal ∂D/∂E at ε_l, HSC 1979). A log-derivative mismatch δD at energy E is therefore worth an eigenvalue error δE_equiv = δD · u(r_d)²/(2∫₀^{r_d}u²), which means the same thing for every channel and element, whereas a fixed radian threshold (δD = (1 + D²)δθ) means a different mHa for a diffuse channel (small u(r_d)²). For O 2p at r_d ≈ 1.7 Bohr (hydrogenic Z_eff ≈ 4.45: u(r_d)² ≈ 0.29, ∫u² ≈ 0.7, D ≈ −1.1) a 0.05 rad mismatch at the window edge is ≈ 22 mHa, and because the first-order term vanishes by norm conservation the mismatch grows ~quadratically in (E − ε_l), so at the ±0.3–0.5 Ha where bonding orbitals sit it is 2–5 mHa — coherent with T3/T4's 5 mHa.

The quantity as first written, δD·u_AE²/(2∫u_AE²), is *not* pole-free: it keeps D^{PP}'s pole wherever u_PP(r_d) = 0 and u_AE(r_d) ≠ 0. The gated form is the Wronskian one in the table, δE_equiv = ½·W(û_AE, û_PP)(r_d). It has no poles (finite û, positive norms). It equals ½(D^{PP} − D^{AE})·√(w_AE w_PP), with w = u(r_d)²/∫₀^{r_d}u², which is the first form times 1 + O(δw), so the two agree to third order at ε_l. Its perturbative meaning is ⟨û_AE|V_PP − V_AE|û_PP⟩ on [0, r_d]; the KB terms cancel beyond β's support. Its magnitude does not depend on the sign of either û. A difference form ½(D^{PP}w_PP − D^{AE}w_AE) is not equivalent. The Wronskian identity gives D′w = −2 for each potential at every E, so that form's slope at ε_l is ½D(w′_PP − w′_AE) with w′ = 2D″/D′². Norm conservation matches D and D′ there but not D″, so that form would measure the mismatch of ∂D/∂E times D, not the energy-equivalent of a log-derivative error (§11). If the classical TM oxygen (f ≈ 1.6) fails T5 at the first run, the task's instruction is to report and have the physics reviewer re-examine the derivation — any threshold change is a spec revision, made before any element-specific result depends on it.

## 5. Integration with the real-space solver

### 5.1 The local part on the grid, and the egg-box

For a pseudised species I at R_I the local potential is split *exactly* into a long-range and a short-range part:

  V_loc^I(r) = −Z_v erf(|r − R_I|/(σ_LR√2))/|r − R_I|  +  V_SR^I(|r − R_I|),  V_SR^I(d) = V_loc^I(d) + Z_v erf(d/(σ_LR√2))/d.

The first term is precisely today's smeared nucleus with Z → Z_v (`nuclear.rs`), with **σ_LR = 2h, a numerical width tied to the grid, not to the element**. Both solvers evaluate it as the **analytic erf per centre**, exact pointwise (`nuclear_potential`, `nuclear.wgsl`, since M3 Task 10), not by a Poisson solve of a deposited Gaussian as an earlier draft said (§11). σ_LR scales with h for a spectral reason: V_LR's content above q_c carries the relative weight e^{−(q_c a/2)²} = e^{−(0.7π√2)²} ≈ 6e-5 at a = σ_LR√2 = 2√2·h, and its alias at 2π/h is ∝ e^{−(πa/h)²} = e^{−8π²}. (An earlier r_loc/3 term was underived and is dropped.) The split is exact, so unlike today's σ it carries **no physical approximation**.

**V_SR is one table** (§11). It is smooth and finite at the origin: V_loc is finite and even, and erf(d/a)/d → 2/(√π a). Beyond r_cmp (§4.3: where |V_ion + Z_v/r| < 1e-8, ≥ max(r_c, r_nlc)) it equals −Z_v erfc(d/a)/d to that tolerance. Its support radius is **R_SR = max(r_cmp, r_floor)**, where r_floor solves Z_v erfc(r/a)/r = 1e-6 Ha (≈ 2.5 Bohr at h = 0.25 and ≈ 3.5 at h = 0.35 for Z_v = 6). Both radii derive from existing ledger constants. Beyond R_SR the table is below 1e-6 Ha, so truncating it there discards nothing of the exact split. An earlier draft split V_SR once more, into V_cmp = V_loc + Z_v/d ("compact") and V_tail = −Z_v erfc(d/a)/d ("analytic, band-limited, never masked"). That split is wrong on both counts. Each piece is singular at the origin (+Z_v/d and −Z_v/d), and only their sum is smooth. And FT[−Z_v erfc(d/a)/d] = −4πZ_v(1 − e^{−q²a²/4})/q² is the *complement* of the band-limited Gaussian part: it decays only as q⁻², so it is not band-limited by σ_LR. V_SR is sampled once per geometry, not per orbital, as `nuclear_potential` samples the erf today. The force uses dV_SR/dd from the same table. The egg-box now comes from three sources, V_SR, the projectors β_lm and ρ_pc, all localised functions sampled on a grid of spacing h.

Fourier filtering by **band completion** (King-Smith, Payne & Lin, *Phys. Rev. B* 44, 13063 (1991); §11). At generation time each radial function g(r) ∈ {V_SR, β_l, ρ_pc}, of support radius R (R_SR for V_SR; r_β for every β_l, §4.6; for ρ_pc where it falls below 1e-12 of its maximum), is spherical-Bessel-transformed on the log grid (a 1-D quadrature — no FFT needed).
- Its transform is kept below **q_c = 0.7 · π/h** (0.7 of the grid Nyquist, universal).
- On (q_c, q_max], with q_max = π/h (the grid Nyquist, derived), the transform is chosen to **minimise ∫_{γ_r R}^∞ |g|² r² dr**, with **γ_r = 1.5**: least squares over a 24-term Chebyshev band.
- The result is transformed back and truncated at γ_r R by a parameter-free C^∞ step one grid spacing wide, which acts only where the optimised tail is already small.

A hard cut at q_c followed by a smooth mask through the cut's Gibbs ringing would re-create high-q content. The plan review measured that construction 10–2000× above the 1e-8 lattice-sum standard, and a crude band-completion prototype reached 1.5e-8 and 1.1e-8 at h = 0.25. The filtered functions are what the grid sees; they are slightly wider (radius γ_r R + h) and band-limited, so their sampling is translation-invariant to the level the completion's leakage sets. The filter is a function of h, so the tables are generated per (Z, recipe, h) — cached, seconds. Two consequences the review added: (S2) the gates of §4.8 certify the *unfiltered* potential, so T1 and T3 are re-run in the radial KB atom with the filtered tables at the scene's h, the filter's own ΔE goes into the generation record, and h_req (T8) is defined from that re-run; (S3) the partial core enters PW92 as ρ_v,σ + ½ρ_pc, and a band-limited ρ_pc = A sin(Br)/r (spectrum reaching ~2π/r_nlc ≈ 10–12 Bohr⁻¹ for O, at or above q_c = 8.8 at h = 0.25) can ring negative in the tail — Task 5 tests min_r ρ_pc^{filtered} ≥ 0 (or ≥ −1e-8 with a documented clamp in the XC kernel).

Plan B (Ono & Hirose, PRL 82, 5016 (1999)): evaluate ⟨β|ψ⟩ on a finer local grid (h/3) inside the projector sphere with ψ interpolated from the coarse grid — the integrand is then band-limited by construction. Kept as a documented fallback for the gate of §6.6 if filtering alone does not reach it. Expected gain of filtering alone, from the mechanism: the O egg-box today is set by a Gaussian of σ = h sampled at h (its k = 1 alias is e^{−2π²} per axis on the *charge*, but its potential and the 1s density it binds are not band-limited); with every ion-centred function cut at 0.7 of Nyquist the residual is the stencil's own translation error on the valence orbitals, the same mechanism that gives H its 4e-5 today. The §6.6 gate (10×, to 3e-5) is set below that expectation on purpose.

### 5.2 The Hamiltonian

  H_σ = −½∇² + V_loc + V_NL + V_H[ρ_v] + v_xc,σ[ρ_v,↑ + ½ρ_pc, ρ_v,↓ + ½ρ_pc],  with ρ_v = Σ_{iσ} f_iσ ψ_iσ², N_v = Σ_I Z_v,I − charge,

V_loc = Σ_I V_loc^I (LR as the analytic erf of every centre — (Z_v, σ_LR) for pseudised species and, for all-electron species under `CoresOnly`, (Z, σ = h) — plus the sampled V_SR fields), V_NL = Σ_I Σ_lm E_l^{KB} |β_lm^I⟩⟨β_lm^I|. The all-electron species in a mixed scene are exactly today's physics; the pseudised ones add V_SR, V_NL and ρ_pc. Energy:

  E = T_s + ∫ρ_v V_loc + Σ_{iσ} f Σ_{I,lm} E_l^{KB} ⟨ψ_iσ|β_lm^I⟩² + E_H[ρ_v] + E_xc[ρ_v,↑ + ½ρ_pc, ρ_v,↓ + ½ρ_pc] + E_ii,  E_ii = Σ_{I<J} Z_v,I Z_v,J / R_IJ

(Z_v = Z for all-electron species). The same "evaluate from the output density and orbitals" discipline as `scf.rs`. `extrapolate_sigma` and the σ ladder apply to all-electron species only; with `IonicPolicy::All` there is no σ anywhere and energies are plain h-convergent quantities.

### 5.3 Applying the nonlocal operator (cost)

Per ion I: the list of grid points inside its sphere of radius γ_r r_β + h (the filtered table's radius, §5.1; r_β = max(max_l r_c,l, r_nlc), §4.6) (rebuilt whenever R_I moves — a bounding-box scan, microseconds), with β_lm^I(r_p) evaluated from the radial table (cubic interpolation) times the real solid harmonic (the Racah-normalised real harmonics already in `qm::poisson`'s multipole code — reused, not re-derived; the surrogate crate's are not used, `qm` stays dependency-free). For each orbital: c_lm = h³ Σ_p β_lm(r_p) ψ(r_p) (n_pts ≈ (4/3)π(γ_r r_c)³/h³ ≈ 2.5 × 10³ points for r_c = 1.4, h = 0.25), then Hψ += Σ_lm E_l^{KB} c_lm β_lm. Cost per orbital per ion: 2 · n_proj · n_pts ≈ 2 · 4 · 2.5e3 ≈ 2 × 10⁴ flops (O: one s projector plus three p for a polynomial local, or just one s for p-local) against the stencil's 25 × N_grid ≈ 5 × 10⁶ at 57³ — under 1 % per ion; a 30-atom scene adds ~10 %. The c_lm are O(N_ions · n_proj · N_orb) scalars: f32 partial sums per sphere finished in f64 on the CPU exactly as `Reducer` does for every other reduction (rule 4).

### 5.4 Forces

Three terms replace `forces.rs`'s single integral for a pseudised ion (the sign chain is that module's: V depends on r − R_I, so ∂/∂R_I = −∇_r):

- local: F_I^{loc} = +∫ ρ_v ∇_r V_loc^I(r − R_I) d³r — the LR part is today's integrand with Z_v and σ_LR, the SR part uses dV_SR/dd from the one filtered table (the derivative of its interpolant, hence the exact derivative of the sampled energy; §5.1);
- nonlocal: with real orbitals, dE_NL/dR_I = 2 Σ_{iσ} f Σ_lm E_l^{KB} c_lm ⟨∂β_lm/∂R_I|ψ⟩ and ∂β/∂R_I = −∇_r β, so **F_I^{NL} = +2 Σ_{iσ} f Σ_lm E_l^{KB} c_lm ⟨∇_r β_lm^I|ψ_iσ⟩** — three more sphere reductions per (orbital, projector), with ∇β from the radial derivative and the analytic gradient of the real solid harmonic;
- NLCC: E_xc depends on R_I through ρ_pc(r − R_I), so **F_I^{pc} = +∫ ½(v_xc,↑ + v_xc,↓) ∇_r ρ_pc^I(r − R_I) d³r**;
- ion–ion: Z_v,I Z_v,J (R_I − R_J)/R_IJ³ as today.

No Pulay term, for the same reason as today: the grid does not move with the ions, and the projectors are explicit functions of R_I whose derivative is taken above. The HF-vs-finite-difference test of `forces.rs` is repeated on a pseudised molecule (§6.6) — the first failing test of Task 7. Because that test's 2e-3 bound cannot see a missing term below it, each term is also checked on its own (§11): at a frozen ρ, frozen orbitals and frozen spin densities, it must equal −∂/∂R of its own energy by finite differences, exact to ~1e-8 and independent of the SCF. The terms are h³Σρ V_SR, ∫ρ V_LR, Σ E_KB c², E_xc[ρ + ρ_pc(R)] and Σ q_I q_J/R_IJ.

### 5.6 Core overlap — the frozen-core honesty surface

The frozen-core approximation fails when the *core densities* of two ions overlap; it does not fail when their pseudisation spheres overlap — overlapping r_c spheres are the normal regime of norm-conserving potentials (TM's own oxygen at r_c = 1.45 Bohr in water, O–H = 1.81), and norm conservation is precisely what makes that valid. An r_c-sum rule would have flagged every bond under `All` (pseudo-H r_c = 1.6 at f = 1.6; H₂ at 1.45 < 3.2) and starved the surrogate of every molecule (review M3). The rule therefore uses a **derived core radius**: r_core,I = the radius enclosing all but a fraction **ε_core = 1e-3** of the N_c core electrons of the AE atom (§4.1), zero for a species without a core (H, He). Hydrogenic estimate for O's 1s² with Z_eff ≈ 7.7: the fraction outside r is e^{−x}(1 + x + x²/2) with x = 2Z_eff r, equal to 1e-3 at x ≈ 11.2, i.e. r_core ≈ 0.7 Bohr — silent for every ordinary bond (O–H 1.81 > 0.7, O–O 2.28 > 1.4), loud when atoms are pushed into each other. The sandbox lets users do exactly that (compression, hot MD). Rule: when R_IJ < r_core,I + r_core,J, a per-step warning "cores overlap: I–J at R = …" in `EvalStats::warnings`, a tint on the pair in the viewer, the step still taken (the potential is finite and smooth — nothing diverges), the surrogate never trained on it. All-electron smeared species do not have this limit (and do have their own: Zσ).

### 6.6 Egg-box gate (the brief's 10×)

Rigid-shift probe as in `validate::surrogate` (8 sub-grid shifts of ±h/4) at 57³, h = 0.25. The gate is stated where the pseudopotential is the only source of egg-box (review M5): **under `All` for H₂O at the relaxed geometry, and under `CoresOnly` for an all-pseudo system (O₂ or CO), max over the 8 shifts of the vector norm |Σ_I F_I| < 3e-5 Ha/Bohr**. The rms energy ripple is reported from the 8 shifts {0, h/2}³, not from the ±h/4 ones (§11). For an inversion-symmetric system the first reciprocal shell enters the energy as cos(2πR_a/h), which is exactly 0 at R_a = ±h/4. It enters the force as sin(2πR_a/h), which is ±1 there, so the ±h/4 force probe itself is maximal and the gate stands. Today's figure of record is −3.4e-4, the y-component of Σ_I F_I at one H₂O geometry (`references.md`), so the probe reports that component too for a like-for-like 10×. Under `CoresOnly` H₂O keeps two smeared H's whose egg-box force `forces.rs` bounds at ≤ 4e-5 each at σ = h = 0.25, so |Σ_I F_I| there is floored at up to 8e-5 (typically a few e-5) whatever the oxygen does: that number is *recorded* against the H floor ("expected ≈ 4e-5, the smeared-H residual"), not gated. Also: the HF-vs-FD force test at δ = 0.01 on OH and H₂O within the `forces.rs` 2e-3 bound (expected far below), and **|HF − Richardson| < 1e-4**, the Richardson estimate being (4D(δ) − D(2δ))/3 with δ = 0.01 (a revision: on a fixed grid HF is the exact derivative of the discrete energy, `forces.rs` derives the HF−FD floor at ≈ 2e-5, and 1e-4 is 5× that; §11). The per-term frozen-state checks of §5.4 come with it. The M4 COM-correction option in `sim` stays, now with a 10× smaller correction to make.

## 7. Cost and risks

**Cost.** Generation: a radial atom is ~10³ log-grid points × ~10 shells × ~30 SCF steps — milliseconds; the gates (KB re-solve, ~300 log-derivative energies × channels, five configurations) and the f/local scans bring an element to seconds on one CPU thread; filtering per h adds a 1-D transform. The grid solve gets *cheaper* than today's all-electron one at equal h (O: 3 orbitals per spin instead of 4; the 1s no longer pins the LOBPCG spectrum); whether h = 0.30–0.35 is usable is what T8 measures per element, not something this spec claims; the nonlocal part is < 1 % per ion (§5.3). Memory: tables of ~10³ f32 per species per channel; index lists of ~3 × 10³ points per ion.

**Risks and mitigations.**
1. **Egg-box on the FD grid** (the brief's main risk): projectors, V_SR and ρ_pc are harder than the valence orbitals. Mitigation: filter at generation (§5.1) with the gate of §6.6; plan B the Ono–Hirose double grid for the projector integrals; the probe is cheap and runs in every validation round. If both miss 10×, the honest fallback is to report the achieved factor and keep the COM correction — never a per-element softening.
2. **Ghost states**: the KB form of a p-local first-row potential is the textbook ghost case. Mitigation: G1 + G2 (§4.6) on every potential, the two-candidate local order, the f ladder; a refused element is reported, not patched.
3. **The universal r_c rule lands too hard or too soft for some element**: too hard → T8 reports an h_req the sandbox cannot afford (the honest answer: "this element needs h ≤ 0.2"); too soft → T3/T5 fail at f = 1.6 and the ladder descends. Both are *outcomes*. The residual risk is that no f passes for some Z — that element is refused at this recipe version.
4. **Spin-polarised transferability (LSDA, open shells)**: the O triplet depends on the NLCC doing its job. Mitigation: T3's Hund configuration is a gate at 5 mHa; the 3-D triplet-below-singlet oracle.
5. **Semicore / two states per l** (Li, Na, Mg): refused until ONCV (stage 3, after this milestone). Li therefore stays the ignored oracle — stated, not hidden.
6. **f32 on the GPU**: c_lm sums over ~3 × 10³ hard-function terms; β near r = 0 is O(1) while ψ is small. Mitigation: f32 partials → f64 finish (rule 4), the gpu_vs_cpu suite at the existing bounds, the Kahan path if the bound is missed (measured, not assumed).
7. **Mixed scenes** (`CoresOnly`: smeared H next to pseudo-O): correct physics for both species but two different approximations in one molecule — documented in §6.4; `All` is the clean state and the intended default.
8. **Threshold mis-set on first contact** (T5/T6): handled by the rule-6 procedure in §4.8 — a spec revision with a derivation, never a per-element exception.

## 11. Plan review (physics review of the implementation plan 2026-10-15, `docs/superpowers/notes/2026-10-15-m7-plan-review.md`; verdict "ready after amendments")

The plan's review found spec errors as well as plan defects. The reviewer's derivations, re-checked here, are folded in as follows (the plan's own amendments are listed in its "Spec questions — resolved" section):

- **M2 / Q2 (§5.1, §5.4, §5.5, §4.9)** — the V_cmp/V_tail split of §10's M4 was singular piece by piece. V_loc is finite at 0, so V_cmp = V_loc + Z_v/d carries +Z_v/d, and V_tail = −Z_v erfc(d/a)/d carries −Z_v/d. And V_tail was not "band-limited by σ_LR": its transform −4πZ_v(1 − e^{−q²a²/4})/q² is the complement of the Gaussian part and decays as q⁻². The replacement: V_LR = −Z_v erf(d/a)/d analytic per centre, and **V_SR = V_loc + Z_v erf(d/a)/d as one smooth table**, filtered and truncated beyond R_SR = max(r_cmp, r_floor), where r_floor solves Z_v erfc(r/a)/r = `v_tail_floor` (1e-6 Ha). The force uses the same table's derivative. No new constant.
- **M3 / Q3 (§5.1)** — "King-Smith's smooth cutoff" read as a hard cut plus a smooth mask. The reviewer measured that construction 10–2000× above the 1e-8 lattice-sum standard, because the mask cuts through the cut's Gibbs ringing. The replacement is King-Smith, Payne & Lin's actual construction: keep the transform below q_c, choose it on (q_c, π/h] to minimise ∫_{γ_r R}^∞|g|²r²dr, and truncate at γ_r R. A prototype reached 1.5e-8 and 1.1e-8 at h = 0.25. The band basis size (24 Chebyshev terms) is a hashed numerical knob in the §4.9 table.
- **Q6 (§5.1, §5.2, §5.5, §4.9, §1.1)** — both solvers evaluate V_LR as the analytic erf (`nuclear_potential`, `nuclear.wgsl`), not by a Poisson solve. So the "Poisson route's ~1e-5 Ha floor" is gone, and σ_LR = 2h is re-justified spectrally: e^{−(q_c a/2)²} ≈ 6e-5 at a = 2√2·h.
- **M1 (§4.1, §8 row 1)** — the hydrogenic check "to 1e-10" is unattainable at δ = 0.008. Numerov's eigenvalue error is O(δ⁴) relative (measured ≈ (0.008, 0.047, 0.17)·δ⁴ for 1s/2s/3s), and the log grid is scale-free, so the absolute error grows as Z²; 7 of the 19 listed states fail (Z = 8 2s: 1.5e-9). The bound is now **1e-10·Z²** (worst margin 2.5×). An order check is added: the Z = 1 1s error ratio between δ = 0.016 and 0.008 must lie in [14, 18], which catches a wrong Numerov weight that an absolute bound would pass.
- **Q1 (§4.1)** — the NIST LSD column is VWN5. VWN5 reproduces H and He to ~1e-6, while PW92 misses the 1e-5 gate by 4× and 38×. The oracles therefore compare like for like on a test-only VWN5 with NIST's spin interpolation. The generator stays PW92, and its totals are reported against NIST (rule 7).
- **Q4 (§4.6, §8 row 3)** — the ghost test "a V_loc with ε₀^loc above ε_s" cannot fail GSS. By the variational principle that case forces E_KB < 0, which GSS calls ghost-free. The two real ghost cases, (A) E_KB > 0 with ε₁^loc < ε_s and (B) E_KB < 0 with ε₀^loc < ε_s, are now stated in §4.6 and tested. Case B needs a narrow well (w ≤ 0.05 Bohr), because the well's share of ⟨φ|δV|φ⟩ grows only as D·w³. G2 is checked on the fixed reference screening, so a ghost that makes the KB SCF fail cannot hide.
- **Q5 (§4.6)** — "no extra bound state … found by node counting" does not hold for a nonlocal operator. G2's count is now the exact inertia count n_KB(E) = n_loc(E) + [s(E) < 0] − [E_KB > 0], with s = −1/E_KB − ⟨β|G_loc|β⟩ = det M/(E_KB·W(u_h, u_i)), by Haynsworth's inertia additivity. The reviewer checked it on 4000/4000 random rank-1 cases. A mesh only locates the roots. The neutral LSDA atom binds one weak (n+1)s, so a channels-only count refuses every element from Be to Ar. G2 refuses an extra state only; a missing AE state is T5's (§4.6).

T5 is now gated on **δE_equiv = ½·W(û_AE, û_PP)(r_d)**, each û normalised to ∫₀^{r_d}û² = 1. It is pole-free, equals the spec's quantity times 1 + O(δw), and has the perturbative meaning ⟨û_AE|V_PP − V_AE|û_PP⟩. The 20 mHa threshold is unchanged.
