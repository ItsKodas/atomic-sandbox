## MUST

### MUST-1 (§3.1 cond. 1–3, §3.2, Task 3) — the accumulation and transverse tests carry a 3–9 % interpolation-error budget, not the claimed ~1e-3
Spec §3.1: "δ = 0.10 sits an order of magnitude above the noise of the ratio (f32 ~1e-6, radial-table interpolation ~1e-3)". Measured — trilinear interpolation on a 0.25 Bohr grid of e^{−κr} (the atomic tail; the error is +κ²h²/8 at a cell midpoint and always positive for a convex tail):

| density | κ | at an axis cell midpoint | at (0.875, 0.125, 0) | at a cell centre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H | 2 | **+3.1 %** | +1.3 % | +0.3 % |
| He | 3.375 | **+9.0 %** | +5.7 % | +4.3 % |

Numerator and denominator both carry such errors and they do **not** cancel: ρ(p*) is one trilinear sample at an arbitrary cell position; ρ_pro is built from 64 trilinear samples per shell (biased up by ≈ ⅔ of the maximum, since the directions land at random cell positions) and then linearly interpolated in r at dr = h/2 (a further +κ²dr²/8 ≈ +0.8 % H / +2.2 % He at a node midpoint). Net: the enhancement ratio is uncertain by several per cent, i.e. ±(0.3–0.9)·δ — and the He₂ oracle asserts `enhancement < 1.0` against a depletion that is itself probably only 5–15 % (see "Not checked"). The transverse test has the same problem: ε_⊥ = 0.02 is *below* the 3 % H interpolation noise between p* and p* ± h (different cell positions); the real H₂ drop of 7 % per h leaves a 4 % margin, not "far below".

**Fix (choose one; put the derivation in `references.md`):**
(a) *Same operator on both sides* — superpose the radial references onto the scene grid at the current nuclear positions (CPU, N_atoms × n³ table lookups, ≈ 1 ms at 49³) and sample ρ_pro at the segment points with the **same** trilinear interpolation used for ρ. The leading error (κ²h²/8 × local curvature) then cancels to the curvature *difference* between ρ and ρ_pro — second order in the deformation density. Build the radial table from **shell-binned grid values** (bin every grid point by |r − R_C|, dr = h/2, no interpolation at all — the atom sits on a grid point at the centre of an odd-n cell) instead of Fibonacci-direction trilinear samples; this also removes the "64 directions" constant.
(b) Keep the current scheme and widen δ / ε_⊥ with a derived budget — not recommended: He₂'s margin is gone.

After the fix, restate the noise line in §3.1, and add a *gridded* fixture to the Task 3 analytic tests (Gaussians evaluated on a 49³ grid and trilinearly sampled) so the bias is exercised, not only the exact analytic path.

### MUST-2 (§3.1, §11 `heh_plus_and_h3_plus_are_bonded`, Task 7) — H₃⁺ is a three-centre bond; the transverse test as written is predicted to reject all three edges
The spec argues only the *accumulation* test for H₃⁺ ("edge midpoints still far above 1 + δ" — true: the exact-atom promolecule at the edge midpoint is 0.085 at the surface's own side 2.117 Bohr (`references.md` "Dynamics (M4)"), against a ρ_b of order 0.1–0.13 → ratio ≈ 1.3–1.5). It never checks the transverse part of condition 1 toward the **ring centre**. LCAO estimate (unpolarised 1s on three protons, ζ ∈ [1.0, 1.4]):

| side | ρ(centroid)/ρ(edge midpoint) | ρ(p* + h toward the centre)/ρ(p*) − 1 |
|---|---|---|
| 1.65 (exact R_e) | 1.07 | **+6.6 %** |
| 2.117 (this surface) | 1.04–1.07 | **+5.4 … +6.1 %** |

Against ε_⊥ = 0.02 that is `NotATransverseMaximum` on every edge — **H₃⁺ gets no bonds and three components**, the opposite of the oracle and of the UI copy "H₃ is not a stable molecule; H₃⁺ is". The real density is polarised (in exact H₃⁺ the ring CP and the three BCPs are nearly degenerate, within ~1e-2 of ρ_b), so the true sign at 2.117 Bohr on the smeared surface is *marginal and must be measured* — and it sits inside the MUST-1 noise either way.

**Fix:** pre-measure the H₃⁺ profile (edge chord + the perpendicular toward the centroid) before the oracle is pinned, and decide the three-centre rule in the spec: either (i) an explicit multi-centre channel — edges that pass floor + accumulation + the along-chord saddle and fail the transverse test *only* toward a non-nuclear interior point where ρ has a local maximum (no nucleus within the rise) are kept as a "multi-centre bond" and labelled so; or (ii) honest reporting: the oracle asserts `components == [[0,1,2]]` only through such a rule, otherwise the H₃⁺ row becomes "reported, not asserted" with the finding recorded (rule 7). The water H–H rejection must survive either choice (there the rise toward O is +13 % per h in the Gaussian model and ends at a nucleus).

### MUST-3 (§3.1 "charged scenes", §11 HeH⁺, §3.6) — HeH⁺ is asserted bonded without a derivation; the neutral promolecule predicts a miss
Exact-atom promolecule along the He–H axis at 1.46 Bohr, 0.9–1.1 Bohr from He (where the chord minimum must lie): **0.23–0.25 e/Bohr³** (He contributes 2 electrons, H 1; the molecule has 2). The actual density there is of order 0.15–0.2 (He-1s donation toward the proton; QTAIM classes HeH⁺ closed-shell-like, ∇²ρ_b > 0). Ratio ≈ 0.6–0.9 → `NotAccumulated`. The spec's own sentence "a cation's bonds can look weaker than they are" predicts this; HeH⁺ is the LiH case, not the H₂ case.

**Fix:** (i) decide the charged-scene rule explicitly: the element-agnostic correction is to scale the neutral promolecule by N_e/ΣZ (the atoms' electrons renormalised to the electrons actually present: ×⅔ for HeH⁺ and H₃⁺, ×10/9 for OH⁻) — document it as a universal, constant-free rule — or document the bias and keep the neutral sum; (ii) in either case move HeH⁺ to the "may miss — reported" group with LiH until measured. With the scaling, H₃⁺'s accumulation margin roughly doubles (ratio ≈ 2); HeH⁺ becomes ≈ 1.0–1.3, still marginal.

### MUST-4 (Task 3 `a_third_atom_does_not_bond_two_neighbours`; §11 water; condition 1) — the pinned fixture does not produce the pinned rejection, and rejects the O–H bonds too
With the plan's numbers (O: A 2.0, w 0.6 at the origin; H: A 0.3, w 0.5 at (±1.43, 1.11, 0); 0.4·g at each O–H midpoint), the exact chord profiles are:
- H–H chord: endpoints ρ = 0.3444, interior minimum 0.3487 at s = 0.78 (and 0.22) → **no interior minimum below the endpoints → `NoInteriorMinimum`**, not `NotATransverseMaximum`. The midpoint is a local *maximum* of the chord (0.418 — O is closest there); §3.1's prose "the segment's minimum sits on the HOH bisector 1.1 Bohr from O" is wrong, the minima are ≈ 0.4 Bohr from each H.
- O–H chord: ρ(O) = 2.05, ρ(H) = 0.3444, interior minimum 0.3545 at k = 31 → **also `NoInteriorMinimum`: the expected bonds {(0,1),(0,2)} come out empty.** The broad O Gaussian's tail at 0.9 Bohr (0.64) plus the bump lift the whole chord above the H peak.

**Fix:** re-parametrise (narrower O, w ≤ 0.35; bump ≤ 0.2·g or placed at the chord minimum rather than the geometric midpoint), evaluate the fixture numerically *inside the test* and assert the variant it actually produces; the water oracle's H–H rejection set must include `NoInteriorMinimum` (on the smeared surface the H–H chord through O's hill is likely monotone from each H to the middle).
The fixture also exposes a design gap in condition 1 for X–H bonds with a smeared heavy X: Kato's cusp makes a nucleus a local maximum of ρ along any line in the *exact* density, but a σ = 0.25 smeared H peak can be overwhelmed by the heavy atom's tail at h = 0.25, so ρ is monotone O → H and the "BCP" degenerates onto the H nucleus. State the rule for that case (e.g. when the chord minimum is the H endpoint, take p* as the innermost sample next to it and run floor + accumulation there) and pre-measure water's O–H chord before pinning `water_has_two_oh_bonds_and_no_hh_bond`.

### MUST-5 (Task 3 interfaces vs Task 7 tests) — rejected pairs carry no numbers, but three oracles assert on them
`detect` returns `Vec<(usize, usize, Rejection)>` with a unit-variant enum, yet `he2_is_a_closed_shell_contact_not_a_bond` asserts `enhancement < 1.0` of a *rejected* pair, `h2_is_bonded_…` asserts the enhancement decreases over R ∈ {1.8, 2.5, 3.5, 5.0} (5.0 is below the floor, hence rejected), and §3.4's hover readout wants ρ_b for rejections. Replace with a per-pair report — `PairReport { i, j, s, rho_min, transverse_max_ratio, rho_pro, enhancement, verdict }` — and keep `Bond` as the accepted subset; `bond_rejections` becomes `Vec<PairReport>`.

### MUST-6 (§4.1–4.3, Task 4) — formula order: the spec's examples and the trivial-table keys are not Hill strings, and §4.2's IUPAC order is misquoted
- Hill (no carbon → *all* symbols alphabetical, H included) gives `HLi`, `HHe⁺`, `FH`, `H₃N`, `HO⁻`, `FLi`, `CHN`, `CH₄O` — the plan's own test pins `hill_formula(&[3,1]) == "HLi"` and `"HHe⁺"`. The spec's examples (`LiH`, `HeH⁺`), the table keys (`LiH`, `HeH⁺`, `HF`, `NH₃`, `OH⁻`, `LiF`, `HCN`, `CH₃OH`) and the labels of §4.4 are conventional order. Under the plan as written `trivial_names_win`'s HeH⁺ assertion **fails** (key `HeH⁺` never matches `HHe⁺`; rule 5 then names it from the wrong element order) and LiH displays as "HLi — lithium hydride".
- §4.2's "IUPAC Red Book IR-4.4.2.1 Table VI restricted to H–Ne: Li, Be, B, C, N, H, O, F, then He, Ne" is misquoted: Table VI puts the noble gases **first** (the most electropositive end): **Ne, He, Li, Be, B, C, N, H, O, F**. That is what makes "helium hydride" and `HeH⁺` come out right.
**Fix:** use one ordering for the displayed formula and name it truthfully — the Table VI sequence above reproduces every example (H₂O, LiH, H₃⁺, HeH⁺, NH₃, HF, CH₄, CO₂, C₂H₆; carbon-first for hydrocarbons falls out of it) — or strict Hill with Hill-ordered keys; and **key the trivial table by composition** (sorted (Z, count) pairs + charge + optional graph signature), never by a string, so a display-order change can never silently break a lookup. Fix the §4.2 citation text and the Task 4 pins accordingly.

### MUST-7 (§6.1, Task 6) — the FIRE backtrack is pinned to an inconsistent formula, and its position change is not booked
- Spec: "r ← r − ½ Δt v_old"; test: "r_after == r_before_that_step + ½Δt·v_old". With velocity Verlet the step's displacement is Δt·v_old + ½Δt²·a_old = Δt·v_half, so the two statements agree only when a = 0. FIRE 2.0 (Guénolé 2020; LAMMPS `halfstepback`) backtracks by half of the *last displacement* (`x −= ½ Δt_old · v` under semi-implicit Euler, where Δx = Δt·v exactly). Define it that way — **r ← ½(r_before + r_verlet)**, i.e. r −= ½Δt·v_half — and pin the test to `r_after == (r_before + r_verlet)/2` to 1e-12.
- The backtrack moves positions without a step, so E_KS changes by E(r_back) − E(r_verlet). The spec books only *velocity* edits; this is a position edit and puts an unbooked jump into E_conserved at every P ≤ 0 event (≈ ½ F·Δr each, 1e-5–1e-4 Ha, accumulating over tens of quenches). Book **W += E_KS(r_back) − E_KS(r_verlet)** (the evaluation at r_back is needed for the next step anyway; E(r_verlet) is already known) and rename `delta_k_booked` → `delta_booked`.

### MUST-8 (§11, Task 7 `fire_relaxes_h3_plus_to_the_quenched_minimum`) — the 2e-3 Bohr side bound is tighter than the stop criterion allows
Both relaxations stop at max|F| < F_TOL = 1e-3 Ha/Bohr on a surface with k ≈ 0.2 Ha/Bohr² (H₂'s pin; H₃⁺'s stretch is similar), so each converged geometry lies anywhere within F_TOL/k = **5e-3 Bohr** per atom of the minimum; two independent stops can differ by ≈ 1e-2 Bohr on a side. The egg-box floor (2.2e-6 Ha/Bohr rms at 49³) is far below F_TOL and does not pin them to one ripple minimum. Either derive the bound as 2·F_TOL/k_softest (k measured and printed), or — better — compare **energies**, which are second order: |E_f − E_q| ≤ N_modes·F_TOL²/(2k) ≈ 1e-5 Ha, and report the sides. The same arithmetic applies to the 5e-3 Bohr R bound in `fire_on_the_surrogate_is_verified_by_the_teacher` (it equals the worst case of the stop criterion alone; make it 1e-2 or tighten F_TOL for that comparison). The `relax_h2` replay window 1.75–1.83 (±0.04) is fine.
