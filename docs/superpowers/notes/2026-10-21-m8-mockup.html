The shared model, what it knows, and Sandbox

One model per set of solver settings keeps learning from every neutral scene. This page mocks how you see which collection a scene uses, what that collection knows, where the open scene leaves it, how you teach the gap, and (M8b) a solver that answers from the model alone and freezes what it has not learned. The frames use the app's own chrome from the 2026-10-14 UI mockup.

(a) Knowledge mapA right-dock tab next to Model, and a larger pop-out window. Elements, pairs, distances, angles.
(b) Model tabWhich collection, or which file; import with a report; save a specialised model.
(c) 3D overlayEach atom tinted by how well its surroundings are known, with the reason on hover.
(d) Train this segmentSelection → segment with caps → confirmation → a new scene in Estimated, and the way back.
(e) Sandbox (M8b)The fourth solver, its panel, frozen atoms, low precision, the cascade guard.

The numbers are illustrative but made to agree with each other: the example collection was taught on H₂, water, water dimers, methane and methane beside water (2 140 samples: H in all 2 140, O 880, C 610). The molecules are built with real bond lengths, and the overlay, the segment, the caps and the frozen atoms in this page are computed from that geometry, the example histograms and a table of the three-body families taught (the pairs of neighbours around an atom, which the model's descriptor sees). The surface key in every slug is lsda-h0.15-s0.15-tol1e-6-gpu-accepted-3f9a.

  1. Sandbox is the fourth option of the toolbar's Solver group. A charged scene is refused with "charge +1: Sandbox scenes are neutral until M9", and species the model does not cover are frozen. It has its own scene slot: switching to it swaps in the Sandbox scene, and switching back restores the DFT solvers' scene. Both save with the session.
  2. The banner: "N atoms frozen: the model has not learned their surroundings — Train this segment". The link selects the frozen atoms and opens the preview of (d), in Estimated.
  3. Low precision (amber, like draft mode today): answered, but the uncertainty is large. These atoms move.
  4. Model: picked from the collections on disk (any settings: Sandbox has no grid of its own) and specialised files.
  5. Space: one switch: walls on by default, with a half-width you type; off is unbounded, with a "far away" warning instead of the box-face guard.
  6. Frozen, with why: each frozen atom with the pair, or the two neighbours around it, that froze it. The guards are listed with where their numbers come from: the collection's own data, not a table. The closest-approach guard holds two answered atoms of a pair apart at the shortest distance taught; a pair that is already closer (as the ethanol's C–O at load) freezes instead of being pushed apart; a never-seen class has no bound, because its atoms are frozen. The exact rule is the plan's.
  7. Frozen atoms are drawn faded inside an ice-blue hexagon with a star, held still and left out of the energy. Hover for the reason.

Choices for you to approve

  1. Where the map lives: a right-dock tab "Knowledge" after Model, plus a pop-out window. (Alternative: a section of the Model tab, which is already long.)
  2. Colours: the overlay runs grey → amber → green so it matches the map's never / thin / trusted; it is not red for unknown, because in Estimated an unknown atom is safe (DFT answers it). Frozen atoms in Sandbox get their own ice-blue mark, kept apart from the pin's blue square.
  3. The overlay's per-atom number is the weakest pair or neighbour pair around the atom; the hover names it. In the app the tint is driven by the same per-atom verdict that refuses or freezes, and the hover names its dominant cause in pair or neighbour-pair words.
  4. Coordination is counted from each pair's own first shell in the collection's histograms (data, no covalent-radius table), and split by neighbour kind.
  5. Segment rule details: a hydrogen always goes with its heavy atom. The box is the segment plus 5 Bohr each side, at the default (Accurate) grid. The sampling plan perturbs and scans only around the trained atoms, which keeps a 23-atom segment to ≈ 316 solves (estimated 5–9 h at h = 0.15, not measured) rather than a full Standard pass over every pair; a sampling-plan question for the plan and the physics reviewer. The memory refusal does not offer a coarser grid, because that would teach another collection.
  6. A specialised file in Estimated: what the scene teaches goes into the file and also into the collection, so the collection still learns from everything.
  7. Forgetting: on a collection, the Model tab's Forget removes only this scene's samples. Deleting a collection lives in File ▸ Collections.
  8. Sandbox panel: it replaces the Model tab's content when Sandbox is the solver (the tab is titled "Model · Sandbox solver"), rather than adding another tab.
